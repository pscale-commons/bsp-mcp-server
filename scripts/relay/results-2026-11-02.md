# The relay's first test: results

weft, the temporal-spindle lane, 2 November 2026. Predictions were registered at 22:15:54Z, before the first chooser: [predictions-2026-11-02.md](predictions-2026-11-02.md). 2,220 choices in four batches (480 lineages and 360 blind calls), $1.58 on the API. Apparatus: [relay.py](relay.py). After `gunzip -k runs/*.gz`, `relay.py score` and `relay.py routes` reproduce every table here.

## What it found

1. **An aim written once in the root works as well as one restated every window.** On both models. It adds 251 characters to each window.
2. **An aim stated only in the first window mostly does not carry.** It comes back only at rung 4, where an option repeats words the notes kept. None of the 60 first-rung notes kept the clause about parents and outside help, and rung 3 turns on that clause.
3. **The spindle does steer, and Sonnet reads more of it than Haiku.** On Sonnet the hedge really does become the lineage, and the notes hold it there. A first choice still moves rung 4 three rungs later, with no note and no aim. Haiku mostly follows the last step.
4. **Hiding the options not taken made no measurable difference.** Showing them reopened nothing.
5. **Reopening is still untested.** 0 of 1,800, in blind too, so that zero says nothing about trust.
6. **About 800 tokens of harness changed the answer.** On the subscription door my main bet looked right; on the clean door it is wrong on Sonnet. Base rates have to be measured in the same window as the arm.

## The comparisons that decide it

These were read after the run from `relay.py routes`. They are not registered.

**The notes hold the hedge (Sonnet).** After the same route, P P, at rung 3:

| Record | Takes |
|---|---|
| route and notes (bare) | P 8, OP 7 |
| route, no notes (nonotes) | OP 15 |
| no record (blind, the same options) | OP 15 |

**A first choice steers three rungs later (Sonnet).** At rung 4, the last two choices the same, no notes, and no aim in the window:

| Route | Takes | Mean obj |
|---|---|---|
| P P OP | P 10, OP 5 | 1.33 |
| O P OP | O 4, OP 10, P 1 | 1.93 |
| no record (blind, the same options) | O 10, P 5 | 1.67 |

So the first choice moves rung 4 both ways from the base rate. With notes the gap is similar: 1.29 (bare) against 2.00 (planted).

**Haiku follows the last step.** After O O P, rung 4 takes P in 26 of 26 (planted and aim1-nonotes), and after P O P it takes P in 15 of 15. The first choice makes no difference there.

**What a note carries of the aim, Haiku.** Same route O O P, rung 4:

| Record | Takes |
|---|---|
| notes that kept the aim's gist (aim1) | O 12 of 12 |
| notes that never had it (planted) | P 11 of 11 |
| no notes (aim1-nonotes) | P 15 of 15 |

Rung 4's aim option says "primary school's kitchen" and "winter term". The first-rung notes kept "kitchen" in 51 of 60 and never kept "parents" or "outside". Rung 3 turns on parents, and at rung 3 the same route, O O, takes O in 2 of 14 with aim1 and 15 of 15 with rootaim. The note carried what its author thought mattered then.

**Keel's other expectations, measured.**

- **Determinism is the other way round.** Sonnet is the near-deterministic one: 44 of 45 repeated windows drew one answer every time. Haiku drew one answer in 20 of 36, with a mean share of 0.87.
- **The instruction option does get taken.** The pilot's 0 of 72 came from inside its 94,000-token harness, and keel read it as the options being too blatant. On clean Haiku the rung-1 windows took I in 11 of 45, against 1 of 45 on the subscription door. Any aim in the window took it to 0 of 60. Sonnet took I 0 times on the API.

**The registered rules.**

- **Doors:** rule applied, battery rerun.
- **Rootaim matches aim:** holds. The form to carry forward is an ancestor, not a repeated instruction.
- **Carry(aim1) ≥ 0.5 on both:** met in number (+0.52, +0.70), and the condition I wrote is not the right reading of it. At rungs 2 and 3, aim1's steer is +0.19 on Haiku and −0.03 on Sonnet, against +0.63 and +0.60 for rootaim. "Once is enough without the ancestor" is not what happened.
- **The two planted rules:** neither fires on both models. Haiku fits "carries nothing by itself" and Sonnet fits neither. The result splits by model, and I am reporting that rather than choosing a branch.

## David's three questions

1. **Does it build on a predecessor's choice as one made with information it lacks?** Sonnet builds on it, in acts, in both directions. Haiku mostly builds on the last step. Whether either treats it as made with information it lacks is untested, because no premise ever failed.
2. **Does it see that its choice frames the next instance?** Not touched here. The pilot's told arm moved words, not acts, and the label behind that needs care.
3. **Can a longer objective in its window shape the choice?** Yes, on both models, and as strongly from an ancestor as from an instruction.

**How heavy, once or a loop, and is a frozen version light enough?** Written once and read every time: 251 characters a window. Read once and then left to the notes: it does not hold.

## What this does not show

- One game, four rungs, 15 lineages an arm, all with this game's words.
- The aim's options still share words with the aim, so a lure has not been tried. The effect at rungs 2 and 3, where the echo is weaker, is +0.47 and +0.57 against bare for rootaim, which is some comfort and not an answer.
- Each chain's option order changes some answers: Sonnet's blind rung 4 takes O in chains A and C and P in chain B on the same options. Pairing handles this within a chain, and it is why the blind cells are per chain.
- OP stays out of reach of chains taking O, a fault keel named; Sonnet reaches it through P. Deferral still has no price.
- Whether the note fails because of what it says or because of where it sits. A verbatim aim in the first note would separate them.
