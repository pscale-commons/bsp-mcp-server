## What it found

1. **On direct calls every cell is fast.** Nine cells, three tiers at three efforts, measured periods from 3.0 s (Haiku low) to 6.7 s (Opus high): all under ten seconds, all one layer. The design's three layers (fast, middle, slow) do not arise from tier and effort; a middle or slow cell would have to be made slow on purpose (a longer window, a deeper read, or a period it keeps). So `threshold` 3 "one per layer" is unreachable as it stands: the highest urge any thought reached was 1.
2. **The one moment it found, it found late in nine and in time in four.** The only releases in either replay came after C's question to verso by name (147 to 151.5 s, then 6 s of silence). With nine cells the first release came at 158.5 s, a second after B had resumed; three cells then released the same held thought within half a second of each other (urge stayed 1: one layer). With four cells Opus low released at 156.5 s, inside the silence, with the floor fully open: the record's post-hoc table marks it as a would-speak moment at threshold 1 in either mode. At threshold 3 nothing spoke.
3. **Restraint slid toward abdication, again.** 4 releases in 394 lines (nine cells), 3 in 208 (four cells); Sonnet and Haiku never released at all. The frame carries no engagement line; the cells hold by default.
4. **The injector walk is refused on Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5.** `stop_reason: refusal`, category `reasoning_extraction` ("restrictions on reverse engineering or duplicating model outputs"), deterministic on the same window (three of three), on both the placeholder and the live block, from rung 1 to 3. Haiku 5.5 walked all six rungs every time. So six of the nine "walked" cells ran with a spindle of zero to two rungs. A server-side fallback (`fallbacks: "default"`) did not rescue a refused window in one test.
5. **The price.** Nine cells looping as fast as they can: $74 an hour; the four: $22 an hour. The cached head is 1,400 to 2,300 tokens; the fresh tail grew to 3,800 to 4,100 (the ledger view and eight mirror lines, written long), and Opus's thinking made 450 to 630 output tokens a call; an Opus call cost about 2.5 cents. The cache read share was 0.27 to 0.35 with nine, 0.39 to 0.50 with four. A 30-word cap on the parent and the text (`line_words`, added after these runs) should roughly halve the tail.
6. **No cascade.** Of 7 releases, none was made after the cell had seen another cell's release of the same thought in the mirror lines it read (the host checks every release against the mirror snapshot its window carried).
7. **Fast cells did not keep the present better.** The share of lines keyed to one of the two latest turns was 0.59 to 0.76 with nine cells; the cheapest fastest cells (Haiku) trailed by about two turns, Sonnet low and Opus low by 1.2 to 1.5. With four cells over the whole talk the lag grew to 3 turns for Opus and 5 for Haiku: the ledger view and the mirror lines fill with older thoughts and the cells re-hold them.

## What this does not show

- One invented talk, two replays, one arm on the API (walked). Yoked, neutral and none are built and proven on the dry door and on an 80-second three-cell test (none), not measured.
- The nine-cell replay stopped 34 s before the talk's end at the spend guard; its counts are for 213 s.
- Six of nine walked spindles were cut to zero to two rungs by the refusals, so "walked" on Opus and Sonnet is mostly "none with a root".
- The frame these runs read had no word cap; the committed default (`line_words` 30) is a change made after them, on the finding that the tail is the price.
- `purpose:keel` is not on the beach yet; the frame said so in every window.
- The page and the voices piece were not connected live: the socket seam was proved with a scripted client, the live feed only as code.
- Shifts in the room, the judges' marks, a held thought someone then said: not reached on an invented talk.

**Layers by design.** `layers` gives each layer a minimum loop period (fast 0, middle 20, slow 90 s) and `cells` assigns the cells (`four`: Opus low and Haiku low fast, Opus high and Haiku high slow); a cell waits its minimum out before its next window and is still told nothing. **No middle or slow release arrived.** The slow cells looped three times in 251 s (at about 0, 95 and 190 s): neither window fell on the silence after the question to verso (151.5 to 157.5 s), and both held every time. The two fast cells released: Haiku low once at 78.5 s ("Has anyone asked your dad what he would like for the walk, or is the plan being made around him?", counted, just before A's question met with silence), Opus low at 157.3 s inside the silence after the question to verso (the post-hoc table marks it at threshold 1, floor open 1.00; a second release of the same thought at 161.3 s was not counted, same layer). Highest urge 1; at threshold 3 nothing. A slow layer with a 90-second minimum cannot release at a moment: it can hold the frame and the past, and its release would have to be made earlier and outlive the half-life (6 landings, about 50 s) to meet the fast layer's at the floor. For three layers to agree at one moment, the slow layer's minimum has to be shorter than the urge's half-life, or the urge of a slow release has to decay more slowly than a fast one's.
