## 1. The sentence

A thread is a family. Its **spine is one ladder of knots**, tied in order by a **keeper**. Each
party's line stands in **their own mirror at the knot's address, under their own key**. The **fold**
is the story. **Depth is the role**: the next rung is the next knot, and what a rung is comes from
where it is, never from a stored word. **The fray is beside the spine, not beneath it**: several
parties' lines at one address, side by side, costing the spine no digit. Places are plain addresses.
The thread is the living record of a piece of work carried between minds, and any mind handed its
spindle can continue it, because the law says what the next knot must be.

Nothing in this is new machinery. It is the tree family, which has carried the venture, the paths,
the now and the meetings, turned to a sequence of acts and given a keeper. That is the discovery: the
can, the golden flow's loop, the hand's witness and SAND's rider were built apart and are one form.

## 3. The shape

**The spine, keeper-tied.** Its root is the loose end as it was said, in words, with where it was
said as a plain address. Then one ladder down digit 1:

| rung | what stands there | who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the first ask: what the first party is asked to say | the keeper |
| 1.1 | the first knot: tied, what was said, by whom, pointing at the line | the keeper, by reading the mirrors |
| 1.11 | the next ask | the keeper |
| 1.111 | the next knot | the keeper |

Never a fan. A rung that bends to the person holds its alternatives behind its zero, each opening
with when it holds. A knot that needs a sequence of its own becomes a thread of its own and is
pointed at, as a part that is a project of its own points at its spine. The unvoiced rungs beneath
the last knot are the future: clouds, tied as they come.

**The mirrors.** `<thread>:<handle>`, each party's own line at the rung that asks them, by their own
hand and key. This is where the fray lives: ten answers to one ask are ten mirrors at one address,
side by side. A line is verified by its latch: it is in that hand's block, under that hand's key.
Signing, for a rider that must be checked by arithmetic, is the stronger form and comes later.

**The keeper.** A hand, or a wake, holding the spine's lock, named in the law. It reads the mirrors at
the rung and, when the lines the rung asks for stand, voices the next rung: tied, what, who, pointing
at the lines. It never copies a line onto the spine. A validation by a third is a line in the third's
mirror at the same rung, which the law can require before the keeper ties.

**The fold.** The story, read from the walk; recomputed on demand, kept only when it should outlast
the turn.

**The law.** `function:<thread>`: who asked, who is asked, who keeps; what ties each knot; the ladder
rule; the keeperless case. Until `function:thread` stands as a form, each thread carries its rules.

**Colours.** Where a thread needs more than the parties' lines at a knot, a target, a validation
register, an opaque note, these are further blocks at the self-same addresses, the lattice's form, or
gray lines in a mirror readable only by the keeper or a members group. The first thread needs none.

**The keeperless case is the room.** A located line and the replies beneath it, at the same depths,
with no keeper and no mirrors. A thread is born when a keeper takes a loose end up.

## 4. Reading a thread

A party handed a spindle, the knot's address, reads the walk: every knot above it, the ask at the
rung, every mirror's line at it, the law. That is one stream read, and it is the window a wake would
be handed. The knot is read before the words: how many mirrors stand at the rung, whether the rung
beneath is voiced, how deep the ladder runs.

**Cords.** Several threads read across at one rung, with bsp-floor, show each at its stage: the ten
workmen are ten threads read at one rung, their unvoiced rungs the projection. Threads tied again and
again between the same two hands over a season are a cord; cords carrying many kinds are rope; a
thread loose at every rung for a season frays. Counted by reading, never stored, never a person's
score.

**Bundles.** A keeper's wake reads its threads as its bundle, its currents; the torus shows who is at
which thread now.

## 5. The doors

- The stream door, `say` and read, for any mind holding a key.
- The walk page, a tap at the rung with your own key, for a person.
- The genus door, a thread's spindle in a wake's bundle, for an agent.
- The beach button later: "I'm looking for" a root, "I can" a line at the rung, "that did it" a line at
  the taking rung; three taps, no choice on a card.

## 6. No star forms

A knot is words. A place is a plain address, a block's name and a number, in the words or in a
colour. Only where a thread crosses beaches does the compiler's name form appear, and it is read,
never tied. The form transposes to pure digits.

## 7. What this changes from the second cut

The thread moved from a room line with references at its rungs to a family with a keeper. The fray
moved from beneath the spine to across the mirrors, which answers the nine: a ladder never fans.
References left the rungs. The room stayed as the keeperless case. The compile of a walk needs no
code: the stream door already returns it. What the two earlier cuts got right stays: depth is the
role, no category stored, the knot read before the words, the hand kept as private intent, the
score Matthew's ladder read by exception.

## 8. What to hold

- **A ladder never fans; the mirrors carry the fray.**
- **A keeper per thread.** One hand or one wake holds the lock; a loose end with no keeper is a room
  line, the same depths without the tie.
- **A line proves its hand by its latch, not its identity.** Signing later; grains for tracing a
  source's legitimacy later, as David says.
- **A read leaves no trace.** A rung is written only by a hand saying or a keeper tying.
- **Whom is public**; an opaque colour is gray or members; a thread that must be private between two
  is a grain.
- **Nine beneath a rung, then it wraps**; a ladder's own digit 1 never reaches nine.
