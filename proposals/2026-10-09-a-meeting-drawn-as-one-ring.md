## 2. The gap David named: instances

Under `cards: spine`, a run's mark lands in the same mirror, at the same address, as that hand's reading of
the design. The second meeting writes over the first. A thought about the design and a record of one day
become the same line, so neither can be read for what it is.

## 3. The ruling — the meeting form (`function:meeting`)

1. **A meeting design is a family, drawn as one ring.**
   - `spine:<name>` holds the design.
     - The root says what the meeting is for.
     - The phases sit at 1 to 9, in the order they are played.
     - A phase's parts sit at its own 1 to 9, also in order.
   - Digit order is the sequence here, as `meeting-protocol`'s own law already reads it. A design is the
     coordinate space every reading and every run answers at, so its phases keep stable addresses side by
     side ([2026-09-24-sequence-is-depth](2026-09-24-sequence-is-depth.md) keeps depth for what is read
     down, such as a document or a record).
   - The minimal numbering is therefore the address itself, and it is a toggle: the titles do the work.
2. **A line opens with its minutes.**
   - The form is the plan, then the range in brackets: `30 (20-40) — THE FIRST WOW: …`.
   - A phase's line ends with its done-when (`Done when …`). The room judges it, never the clock.
   - A line with no minutes stands outside the hour, as a door before or after it.
   - A part with no minutes shares its phase's time equally.
   - One design serves any length: ten minutes, an hour or three hours keep the same shares.
3. **Readings are mirrors.**
   - What a person thinks of the design goes in their own mirror, `<name>:<handle>`, at the address it
     answers. That covers a comment, a change they would make, or their own name for a part.
   - A reading that opens with minutes is also an estimate. The page lays the estimates at a part beside its
     plan, with their middle value.
4. **A run is a dated mirror, never a copy.** This is the answer to David's question.
   - One meeting played on the design is kept at `<name>:<beat>:<handle>`, at the design's own addresses.
     - `<beat>` is the sundial address of the beat the meeting began in.
     - `<handle>` is the hand that kept it. It comes last, so the beach binds the name to that hand's key.
   - The root says when the meeting ran, the length planned, the time taken and who was there.
   - Each phase's and part's line opens with the minutes it took. A phase's line goes on to say whether its
     done-when held (yes, partly or no) and what happened.
   - Two hands at one meeting keep two runs at one beat, so the meeting's two renditions sit at one cell, as
     `meeting-protocol` 4.1 asks.
   - A run stands at the design's addresses, so any run lies against the design and against every other
     run. Pages that list a family's mirrors as `<name>:<handle>` with no second colon (as /portal does)
     leave runs out of the readings by construction.
5. **A run deals cards; it does not play the design as a deck.**
   - A commitment made in the meeting becomes a card in its maker's own hand, `ahead:<handle>`.
     - Its sentence is the commitment.
     - Its 2 is the family it serves.
     - Its 5 is the run's address it was dealt from.
   - Done, the card lands in its holder's mirror as the card law says, and the next meeting's run witnesses
     it.
   - The next meeting itself is a booking, said at its time in the now family.
6. **The fold at the bare name** is the synthesis. At each address it says what the readings and the runs
   say together:
   - what the part is for, as people now read it;
   - what it actually takes;
   - how often its done-when holds.

   Anyone may make it and anyone may supersede it, and it never stands over a mirror. The design changes only
   when its keeper changes it, archived first.
7. **The page.** happyseaurchin.com/ring/<name> draws any family whose law says `mode: meeting`.
   - **The ring**:
     - phases on the inner ring, clockwise from the top;
     - parts on the outer ring;
     - titles along the arcs, with numbering a toggle;
     - before-and-after doors above the start.
   - **A tap on any slice opens**:
     - its words and its done-when;
     - every hand's reading there, and each run's line there;
     - the synthesis;
     - a box for one's own reading.
   - **Run mode**:
     - a hand moves on the clock;
     - a mark is made at each change of part;
     - an outer ring shows what each part actually took against the plan, with any overrun past the full
       circle;
     - at the end the run is kept as the dated mirror, under the keeper's own key.

   "Forms of ring, like the forms of /walk" are the families: every meeting design is one, all drawn by the
   same ring.

## 4. The first instance — the action cycle

`spine:action-cycle`, David's design of 9 October, in his words:

- **1 Pre-meeting.** Who comes, where and when.
- **2 Game rules**, 10 minutes (5 to 10):
  - welcome and the wows' objectives (the green card);
  - the rules, where breaking one, anyone lifts the yellow card;
  - the game ends, at the hour or on a red card.
- **3 First wow**, 30 minutes (20 to 40):
  - sharing issues;
  - a kernel that's interesting;
  - the state change, once something becomes obvious to the people in the room.
- **4 Second wow**, the remaining 20 (10 to 20):
  - voicing what people are willing to do;
  - serious commitment;
  - the decision that confirms it.
- **5 Post-meeting.** Each commitment carried by the person who made it.

The plan of 10, 30 and 20 is weft's reading of David's ranges: each phase sits at a point in its range, and
together they make the hour. It stands at `action-cycle:weft` for him to correct.

## 9. A meeting arranged from a design (10 October)

> "a bunch of people are trying to arrange a meeting and it's actually just to play the role-playing game … 10
> minutes of creating a character and landing, and then half an hour of playing. And then 20 minutes of review …

> actually is going to occur as mirrors … how do we create a specific meeting of the RPG type? … so that I can
> share that with other people … this RPG … would be a specific RPG called First Test. First Online Test."

- **A design and an arranged meeting are different things.**
  - A design is how a kind of meeting runs: the action cycle, invest-forward, the role-playing game.
  - An arranged meeting is one occasion of a design: First Online Test, or an action cycle at one building or
    company. It has its own people, its own time and its own record.
- **An arranged meeting is a family with no spine of its own.** Its law says `mode: meeting` at 1 and
  `design: <design>` at 2, and the ring draws the design's spine by reference, so nothing is copied.
  - Its readings stand in each person's own mirror, `<meeting>:<handle>`, at the design's addresses. The
    pre-meeting is where people first say when they can come and who is coming, as readings, so no room is
    needed.
  - Its fold says where things stand, for example the time most can make, and its runs are dated mirrors at its
    own name.
  - It is shared by its own link, happyseaurchin.com/ring/<meeting>.
  - Its line stands at `function:meeting` 8 beneath its design's line, so the ring's selector offers it and the
    design's page lists it.
- **The role-playing game** (`spine:role-playing-game`, `function:role-playing-game`, under David's key) is
