## 1. One record, three views

The keeper's AI writes the meeting once. Three readers draw it:

- **The ring** (`/ring/<name>`) draws the hour's shape, for running a meeting.
- **The standard page** (`/met/<name>`) draws it for the people who were there.

The record is four blocks: `spine:<name>` (the meeting as held), the fold `<name>` (the account), `function:<name>` (its law, its page), and each person's reading `<name>:<handle>` (their answers). Talk lands in the keeper's own room.

## 2. The standard page, rebuilt like a proposal

- **Side by side.** The drawing is on one side, sticky on a wide screen; the words are on the other. On a phone they stack.
- **The drawing tells the story.** The people's strands, one per person and coloured, come down from their names and twist into one cord. Each thing talked about is a knot on the cord, sized by its minutes. Each answer is a small knot beside it in the answerer's colour: filled for a yes, a ring for anything else. The next steps hang on below, dashed, beyond the hour. Each knot has its words beside it, with a **words** switch to hide them. Tap a knot and its row opens; scroll the words and the knot of the part being read lights.
- **The head** carries Easy / Normal / Detailed, **Snazzy ↗** (where the law names a crafted page), **The ring ↗**, and **Share**. Share gives the crafted page when there is one, as a link and a QR code, with the plain page beneath.
- **The account comes from the fold, about everyone there.** A meeting kept before the fold held one still reads the keeper's reading, as the first version did.
- **The recording's quality.** Where the recording's line says the transcript did not label its speakers, a note by "What we said" says that who said what is the AI's reading, and invites a correction.
- **Talk.** It goes to the room the meeting's Where names, else the keeper's parlour. A line there carries `spine:<name>` at its 2, so the page shows only this meeting's lines, and the keeper hears them where they already listen. The router reads a 2 that is not a digit address as outside any located view (`atOrUnder`), so a parlour's located reads are untouched.

## 5. `ways:met` — the way any AI makes one

It is written like `ways:plug`: everything an AI needs, as text only, so an AI in any app with the beach connector and its person's key can follow it with no code from us. Its steps:

1. **Get the recording** through the app's connectors (email, Drive, or pasted in). Otter has no connector: export it as text, or let Otter email it. Note whether the speakers are labelled.
2. **Name and found the meeting** as `<guest>-<day>-<month>`, never a bare person's name, under the keeper's own key: the spine in the meeting form's shape, with five to seven talk parts with their minutes and the steps; its law.
3. **Write the account into the fold**, about everyone there, with the evidence one rung beneath. Link only what the AI has opened and seen to be true. The recording's line states the transcript's quality. Leave out personal circumstances, money between people, remarks about absent people, and language.
4. **Show the person before anything lands**, then read each block back.
5. **The keeper's own reading** holds only their answers.
6. **Hand back** the link (`?plex=1`) and the QR code. Talk reaches the keeper's own room.
7. **The snazzy page** is a proper build, code, made the way crafted proposal pages are made (`ways:proposal`). Ask for it in one line at pool:weft; `function:<name>` 6 names it once it stands.
8. **Several people:** WHO names them all, the account is about all of them, and with an unlabelled transcript attribute only what the words make plain.

So Matthew, after a meeting: *"Make the meeting page for my conversation with X, from the transcript in my email, the way ways:met says."* His Claude, with the bsp connector and his key, lands the standard page in a few minutes. The crafted page follows from a session that holds the site.
