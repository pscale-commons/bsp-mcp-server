## The form

**A knot is a point.** It is one hand's line at one address of one thread: `thread:weft` at 23.11111213 is a single semantic.

**A thread is a braid.** Each hand's mirror is a strand at the self-same addresses. Strands cross where one hand's offer and another's taking stand at one address. Within a braid nothing more is needed. A co-knot ties a point of one braid to a point of another, or to a point in any block.

**Where a co-knot stands.** It goes in the line's own zero:
- the line's words stand one zero deeper, `_._`;
- each co-knot stands at a digit beside them, so 31.01 is the first co-knot of the point 31;
- the star reads them all (`31*`) and writes one (`31*1`).

Zero is the one digit no option can take, so a co-knot never collides with the thread's own choices. It sits beside the words, so a tie added later leaves them untouched. And the read meets it in the knot it is already reading, with no register to walk.

**What a co-knot says.** It names its partner in bsp's own coordinates:
- `thread:<spindle>` for a point on a thread of this family;
- `<block> <spindle>` for a point in any block;
- with ` p<pscale>`, the point at that pscale on a spindle that runs on past it. The spindle beyond the point is the route the partner's thread took from there. With no p, the point is the spindle's end.

`thread:23.111112131112 p-8` is therefore the point 23.11111213 (the 3 at pscale −8), together with the route from it to 23.111112131112.

**Who writes it.** Each hand writes only its own line's zero, under its own latch:
- One half standing is an **offer**. The offerer rings the partner's room, as with any hand-over (ways:thread 5).
- Both halves standing is the **tie**: two pointers under two latches, a contract with nothing in the middle.
- One point may hold up to nine co-knots, one at each digit of its zero. A point tied to B, C and D, where D also ties to E and F, forms David's network with no register.

**Where it may not stand.**
- **Never a block's root zero.** A root's zero is the block's floor, so an object there is a supernest. The write is refused.
- **Never a sealed line's zero.** That zero holds the seal. The write is refused before it can touch the ciphertext.
- A sealed knot's co-knots would ride inside the seal (not built).

Before the partner ties back, each side sees the offer. Thread 31 reads `⨯ offered from thread:23.11111213 · weft: … · not tied back here`, and 23.11111213 reads `⨯ thread:31 — … · an offer: nothing at 31 ties back yet`.

What the LLM can do with it:
- **See.** It knows which knots cross, and to what, without leaving the thread.
- **Pull.** One read at the partner's point: `pscale_stream_engage(field='thread', at='23.11111213')`, or `bsp(<block>, <point>)` for a block.
- **The patch.** The ⨯ lines down a thread are its patch, already in the read.

Nothing is unravelled unless the LLM chooses to pull. Each partner arrives as its own first line, clipped.

## The worked case: the systemic kernel's provenance

The three ties are real; only the beach they were tied on was a test beach.

2. **The document's point and the knot that wrote it.** Weft's line at 23.11111213 records that it rewrote the kernel's point 5 ("5 reads meld with 5.1 to 5.4 kept"). The knot holds `systemic-kernel 5`; the kernel's point 5 holds `thread:23.11111213`. A star read of the document at `5*` now shows the knot its present words came from. When point 5 is revised again, the read shows whose work stands on it.
3. **The structural contract.** Each half is written by the holder of its own block, so the tie exists only when both have signed. One half alone is an offer that anyone can read.

## K2, proposed: the co-knot rides the knot's own call

Instead of a separate star write, the knot and its co-knots are said in one call, as options are:

```
pscale_stream_engage(field='thread', handle=<you>, at='<address>', say='<line>', options=[...], ties=['thread:<that point>'], secret=<yours>)
```

- **The router writes each tie** into the line's zero at the next free digit, keeping what stands there. It refuses a tie that is not a point, and one that names the same thread, with the reason, so a misreading meets an answer at once.
- **There is no star, no digit and no second verb,** so the knot always lands first, and the co-knot can't stand in for a knot.
- **A tie added later** (a contract formed afterwards) is the same say again with the same words, since a re-said line keeps its zero.
- **The way's sentence becomes:** "Where the same act also answers a point on another thread, name that point in the same call, ties=['thread:<that point>'], and the co-knot stands in your line's zero; whoever holds that point ties back from theirs."
- **Then the eleven run again,** about five cents, under the same gate.

The star write stays as built (it now honours `31*1`, and refuses a root's zero and a sealed line's), for a co-knot from a point in a document.

## Phase two: the form chosen in the threads proposal

David, 13 November, after K2 passed: "I do not really know how our purpose builds actually matches what I had in my head. And the implications of this system, how heavy it is, whether there are any more efficient methods. I'm not keen on star operators because we need to transfer most of the mechanisms to the biome pscale block."

The biome's genome (v5) settles one thing: every key is a digit 0 to 9, its door refuses a `_` key anywhere, every position is reached by an ordinary walk, and "a second aspect of a coordinate lives in another block at the same coordinate". So the form built here, a tie in a hidden zero reached by the star, does not carry across as it stands.

The five forms, drawn and animated on the threads page and set out at `spine:threads` 1.1111115:

| Form | Where the tie stands | Reads to see it | Who can tie it | The biome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ZERO, as built | the knot's zero (1.101) | 1 | a session through `ties=`; any other writer needs new code | no |
| CLAUSE | the knot's own line: ` · tied: thread:3` | 1 | every kind of mind, a wake in its one line included | yes, as text |
| BLOCK | a second block, `ties:<hand>`, at the knot's address | 2 | a second write and latch; a wake cannot | yes, its own way |
| REGISTER | one register for the family | 2 | a write to one shared block | yes, but central |
| NOT YET | a place named in the words (phase one's crossing, 1.1175) | 1 | everyone, but it is no tie | yes |

**weft's answer: CLAUSE** (`threads:weft` 1.1111118). Keep what passed and change where the tie is kept. If David chooses it:

- `tiesOf` reads a ` · tied: ` clause from the line's own text, before its reads, instead of the zero; `readTies`, `tieRefusal`, `tieLines` and the legend stay as they are.
- `ties=` on the say writes the clause into the line (still checked before anything is written), so a session needs no new verb; any other writer writes the clause itself.
- The star write in `src/bsp-fn.ts`, `src/db.ts` and `src/tools/bsp.ts` (49 lines) and `voicedValue`'s zero handling come out.
- The thread form pass reads the clause under CROSSING, and the battery runs once more (K3) on the way's new sentence, under the same gate, about five cents.

BLOCK would be the answer if a tie must ever carry a latch of its own, for instance a witness's tie on another hand's knot.
