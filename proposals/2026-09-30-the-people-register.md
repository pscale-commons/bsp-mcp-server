are using the same reference image — there's no diversity … we are not maximising the pscale

The cause was on the beach, not in any seat. Brackenfoot's registers held:

## 2. What stands now

**One block, an aspect of the places register.** `people:<world>` is to the lens what
`identity:<world>` is to a standing: every address is `spatial:<world>`'s own, a person at the
place they keep, and the block is senseless alone. Where spatial has a room with a figure in it,
the person stands at that room's address (the sergeant at 132, the smith at 141, the burner at
222); where a room holds several, they take digits beneath it (the alewife 121.2, the two soldiers
of the settle 121.31 and 121.32, the smith's wife, daughter and son 142.1–3); where a person keeps a
building and no one room, they take a free digit beside its rooms (the reeve at 133 — the
pass-through rule of the identity aspect).

**A person is one spindle from wide to close.** This is the whole design, and it is pscale doing
what it does: depth is the scale of looking.

```
121.2     The alewife at her trestle — lean, grey-aproned, head down …        across a yard
121.21    At her work she sets a cup down and draws from the barrel …          the body at its work
121.211   At arm's length — a narrow, closed face lined about the mouth …      the face, the hands
121.2111  Pressed, the cloth stops and her hands lie still on the board …      the face under pressure
```

A shot reads a person down to the depth its framing needs and no deeper: a figure in the distance
is the person's own line; a close-up walks three steps down. Every rung is true of everything
beneath it, so a walk to the deepest rung arrives with the whole person above it. Fans are small
and have room: a second way of standing, a second expression, are siblings waiting to be written.

**One man keeps one face.** The levy rotates its posts, so the watch at the ford, the guard at the
Store and the guard at the diggings are not new people: each is a line that ends in a reference to
the soldier who usually draws it (`*:<origin>:people:brackenfoot:121.31`). Two people are two
addresses and never share a likeness; one person at two posts is one address.

**Only what is seen.** Why a person does what they do is the author's, in the notes; a name and
what it means is identity's, at the same address, earned in play. Neither is carried here. The
register is the observer's: `frame-spec:brackenfoot` names no people block, so it enters no
character's frame.

## 3. Why a sibling block, and not deeper in spatial

The keeper's authoring law puts appearance in spatial's public face and marks each standing figure
"extend by subnest when play reaches it". Deepening the figures where they stand was the first
reading, and it is wrong for this purpose: the medium tier reads a place "faces only, two rings
down" (`src/tools/tiers.ts`), so visual detail written two rings beneath a room would ride into
every resolution call at every live table, and deeper detail would be invisible to the lens's own
walk from the room. Separation by block is the precedent the registers already set — identity and
the notes are siblings on spatial's addresses "so every read shape of the public blocks is safe" —
and it keeps play's reads exactly as they were on the day two tables were mid-scene.

Spatial's one-line glance stays what a character perceives. The people register is what a lens
reads. They agree, because the register's first line for each person was written from spatial's.

## 4. Where the lines came from

- **Canon first.** Every person and every first line is from `spatial:brackenfoot` and
  `identity:brackenfoot`: the factor knee-high and hunched, grey-toothed, wetting his fingers; the
  sergeant vast, red-faced, gone to fat; the alewife lean and closed-faced; the smith grey and
  burn-scarred; the reeve stooped and sleepless; the burner weathered and spare with words; the boy
  of sixteen on the Store's day-watch; the two hard-cases scarred and bored on the settle.
- **The record, where it had shown more and did not contradict.** The open table's beats gave the
  sergeant his half-buttoned coat and his bad knee, the factor his wet voice and tapping fingertip,
  the burner his hand flat on the turf and his standing off the left side, the alewife her cloth
  and her pointing chin, the smith his palm on the anvil.
- **New, at David's word.** Faces, hands and pressed expressions for everyone; the looks of the two
  soldiers of the settle and of the tall soldier, whom canon names only by their bearing; the
  levy's kit as "heavy coats no two alike", which makes sameness a fault by the register's own
  first line.

**Divergences found at the open table**, left as that table's own: its narrator named the
sergeant "Vane" where canon holds another name; it drew the alewife stout where canon says lean;
and it has shown more soldiers than the canon crew. The register follows canon, and the recipe
tells a seat to read who is who by role and place.

## 5. How a seat uses it (`ways:stills` 6.6)

Every person of the place named or shown in a moment is read in the register and nowhere invented,
down to the depth the framing needs. A person's reference picture is the newest entry, in any
maker's book, addressed to their address in the register — exactly as a face is addressed to a
character's look at `passport:<handle>:3`. A figure with no reference yet is drawn from their lines
alone, unlike every face already in frame. Player characters are read as before. The people come
after the place and before the look.

Mark Beall's seat (Limner) was told in the working room (`pool:onen-rpg` 19) with the addresses.

## 6. What this does not do, and what follows

- **The gallery page does not read it yet.** Its composer knows only the player characters (the
  passports at the table). Reading the register by role and place is the next step, and it belongs
  with the compiled shot below rather than as one more prose match in a page.
- **The compiled shot.** A shot is a bundle of addresses — the beat, the place's line of descent,
  each person in frame to the depth the framing needs, the look — and the router already compiles
  bundles into one window (`src/compile.ts`). One compiled shot for every seat replaces each seat
  assembling its own prompt from the recipe's prose.
- **The fold law.** Anyone may add what they see in their own mirror at the same address
  (`people:brackenfoot:<handle>`, the genome's mirror law). What enters the register is the
  author's copy, by hand, today. A function block stating whose mirrors fold, or the hard tier
  folding on a schedule, is the move after.
- **No reference pictures were made.** Making one per person costs a call each on somebody's key,
  and no spend was asked for or given.
- **Other worlds.** Thornmere, Threshold, Coldcote and the Hollow King hold the same three
  registers and no people block. The shape transfers as it stands.

## 7. Relation to "each figure speaks for itself" (#459)

The same people, another aspect. That proposal asks how a standing figure speaks and what it can
know from where it stands; this register says how it looks. Both address a figure by the place it
keeps, and neither needs the other to be built first.
