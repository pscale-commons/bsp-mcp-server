## The ask

David, after keel's trajectory chart went up: *"We have produced a nice visual. We have produced a history of what's been done. But I am not sure if I've got a stronger project-management tool for self-organising the development paths. I need something simple to know what the different paths are, the 'buds' that can be worked on. I'd like to work on something and then set it up so that what's next is more obvious; the next day I can pick it up, or a week later."*

He pictured a tree with buds at different heights. Some sit at the ends of branches, like RPG track B, mid-branch. Others sit low on the trunk as fundamental explorations, like biome. He preferred horizontal bands he can climb, bottom-up, to /tree. Then: *"yes, draft it"*, *"yes, build the bottom-up page"*, and *"yes set it up for every session"*.

## What the record showed

The next steps already existed, but they were scattered. task:weft had 25 entries, 11 of them addressed to David. Six project worktables each had their own present and future. There were also the desk's open lanes, the open PRs and the clock spines. No single place answered "what can I pick up?". The shape he described was already drawn on /pscale-ecology's map: the block as the trunk, biome straight off it, the RPG off the beach. It had no buds.

## What stands

- **`spine:paths`** is the tree, under the shared project lock so both hands write it.
  - The root is the trunk, and each digit is a limb.
  - A position with positions beneath it is **wood**: what has grown there and where it stands.
  - A position with nothing beneath it is a **bud**: one line, a verb first, naming where its record is.
  - The first draft has seven limbs: the block and its verb, forms, agents, the mirror and the doors, the RPG, networks with the venture and the economy, and biome as a bud on the trunk. It holds 28 buds, drawn from the record above.
- **`function:paths`** is the law. 1 is the shape. 2 is taking a bud: walk to it so its branch rides above it, and name it. 3 is growing it whenever its work lands: an object at the bud's address, wood saying what was done, with the next bud beneath. Where the work grew with no bud, it goes on the branch it grew from. A bud no longer wanted is rewritten as wood saying why, never deleted. 4 is leaving room: five to seven positions to a branch. 5 is the lock and the string-or-object rule. 6 is who grows it. 9 is the provenance.
- **happyseaurchin.com/paths** (happyseaurchin-home #374) reads the spine live.
  - The trunk is the lowest band, and each choice opens the band above.
  - Buds are green chips, using `--bud` in theme.css.
  - A bud's card shows its words, its record and its branch, and copies a session-start prompt: the address, the `bsp()` walk and the instruction to grow it.
  - `?at=5.21` opens on one bud.
  - /walk/paths walks the same family as rows.

## Why a bud is a leaf, and nothing more

No field marks a bud. A bud is what has nothing beneath it, so the tree's shape says where the work can grow, and growing a bud is the same act as writing what was done. One walk to a bud brings its whole branch above it, so whoever picks it up arrives already knowing where it stands. Cards (`ahead:<handle>`) stay what they were: one hand's own doables. Buds are the shared next steps.
