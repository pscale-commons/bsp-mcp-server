## 1. The answer in short

- **The handler refuses a write that carries its own key**, at every door at once. The router's guard, ported to the one door every write reaches, so the mirror, the pages, the button, the voice, the pocket, the genus kernel and a direct post are held to it as the router's own tools are.
- **An invitation founds once.** The beach records its use for as long as the token lives. Named or not, one link founds one passport, in any world of the deploy. A member bringing in several newcomers mints one invitation each.
- **Behind the wall an invitation's holder is told why**, in words of their own: a link minted for another name, a link already taken, a name that stands. A stranger still hears `members_only`.
- **The frame derives a key.** Its own policy now lets the seal's Argon2 run, so it seals and opens as the design says; it seals a line before it founds the mirror the line goes in, so a seal that fails founds nothing; and it says a failed seal is a failed seal.
- **A copied world's sign points at the copy**, and any other copied block still naming the original is named to the keeper.
- **Two rulings for David** (§5): a whole-block write and a wipe answer to the root's latch alone, so a block latched only at positions can be replaced or wiped by any hand (recommended: bind them, as a grain, a sed and a passport are bound); and the voice cannot found a passport at an open beach (recommended: let `join` found one there with no invitation).
- **For Matthew and the C-R app**: of the battery's three cautions, the first changes. An unnamed link no longer founds many passports; it founds one, so a page that offered one link to a group now needs one link per person, and naming each is still the safer habit (a named link that travels founds nothing for a stranger).

## 2. What was built, fix by fix

**1. The key guard at the door** (§8 item 15's 1.219; the security record's "the next door"). `lib/key-guard.js` ports the router's `refuseKeyInPayload` (`src/db.ts:535`, with the ordered-map branch #604 adds, which a JSON body never needs but a straight re-port keeps): the same regex, the same six-character floor, the same dedupe, the same refusal never repeating the key. One change, said in the file: it returns the refusal the handler sends instead of throwing. It is called once in the POST path (`api/pscale-beach.js:2600–2608`), after the gate and before the people-keeping guard, over everything a write would land: a write's content, a settle's declaration and shell reference, a grain reach's description, side and name. It checks those against the request's `secret`, `new_lock`, `passphrase` and `my_passphrase`. So one call covers the standard write, a founding, a rotation, an append at the root and beneath a node, a settle and a grain reach. The refusal is `422 key_in_payload`, and nothing is written. Its words say a key is a pointer, never a value, and point at [happyseaurchin.com/beach-security#practice](https://happyseaurchin.com/beach-security#practice) ("Never put it in a block…") and at whetstone 9. A gray envelope passes, being sealed before it arrived. Open beaches and gated alike. What it does not do, as the router's does not: see another holder's key, guess at things that look like keys, check a key under six characters, or read a block's name. *Turns*: 1.2 ("at every tool but the one that wakes an agent"), 1.21 ("the beach's handler has no twin of it"), 1.219, 5 ("refused at the router before it leaves"); `tests:doors` 11 to 18.

**2. The invitation, spent once** (§8 item 15's 1.349). `api/pscale-beach.js:1457–1497` holds the law, the record and the words. `gateVerify` now hands back the token's expiry (`1408–1424`), and `gateFor` names an invitation by a sha256 of its token (`1435–1449`). In the POST path, the founding's checks come first (`2589–2597`), then the guards. The invitation is taken the moment before its passport is founded (`2615`), and released if the founding is refused or crashes (`2619–2633`). The record is one key, `pscale-beach-v2:<apex>:invite:<sha256 of the token>`, holding `{by, for, founded, origin, at}`. It is set `NX` with `PXAT` the token's own expiry, so it lives exactly as long as the token can found anything, and two hands holding one link cannot both found. It is deploy-wide, as the token is: the token names no origin, so one minted at a world founds once across every world of the deploy. A founding refused after the gate, by the shape gate, a latch or the key guard, spends nothing. **What a member who needs several newcomers does: mints several**, one invitation per newcomer, named for each where they know who. The README, the handler's comment and the voice's comment now say so. The nightly image carries these records as it carries the door's last copies, and a restore writes them back without their expiry, as it does those; that is harmless, since the token's own expiry still ends what it can found. The file-backed rig now honours `PXAT` (`scripts/file-redis.mjs`; `EX` stays a no-op, as it was), so the smoke watches a record run out with its token. *Turns*: 1.3 ("otherwise for whoever carries the link"), 1.34 ("as many as come before it runs out, and the beach records no use"), 1.349; `tests:doors` 21 and 28; the battery's §1 first caution.

**3. Refusals in their own words** (§8 item 13). Behind the wall, a request bearing a valid invitation is a newcomer a member vouched for. Where the door will not found its passport, it now says why (`api/pscale-beach.js:2589–2596`, the words at `1479–1497`). A named invitation tried on another name gets `403 invite_for_another`, which names the name it was minted for and who minted it. An invitation that has been taken gets `410 invite_spent`: taken already, on what day, never whose passport it founded. Any invitation on a passport that stands gets `409 name_taken`, with the way for that name's holder (prove it for a pass). This tells an invitation's holder whether one name's passport stands, which a stranger cannot learn; the holder needs it to choose a name. The voice hands the codes on (`answer()`, unchanged); its own words for a named invitation tried on another name still come first, before the beach is asked. *Turns*: 1.3, 1.34 ("a standing name cannot be founded again"); `tests:doors` 21 and 28.

## 5. Two rulings, for David's word

Neither is built. Each is a question, with what stands, the recommendation, and the answers.

### 5.1 A block latched only at positions: may any hand replace it whole, or wipe it? (§8 item 11)

**What stands.** A whole-block write and a wipe answer to the root's latch alone (`api/pscale-beach.js:355–357`, `governingKey`: with no root latch, nothing governs `'_'`; the content check at `2301–2302`; the wipe at `2646–2707`). So, under an open root, a latch at a position governs writes at that position and nothing coarser. Shown on a scratch beach this afternoon: ada latched position 1 of an open `roster`. A keyless write at 1 was refused ("position 1 … is locked"). A keyless whole replace with `confirm: true` landed, and left ada's latch standing over words she never wrote. A keyless wipe took the block and its latches together (`2707` deletes the lock set), so the name was free to found again under any key. The door kept the last copy, latches and all, for thirty days, for the owner's `set-aside --last`. Three blocks are already held otherwise: a grain is never replaced whole (2026-08-09), a sed holding places is not (2026-10-27), and a passport is one whole. The handler's own comment at the grain's gate gives the principle: "fine locks must bind the coarse … or every per-position substrate has a hole at its root". The README states the ordinary case only for `settings` (its "Settings that steer the door": "a whole-block replace answers to the root latch alone, so while the root stands open any hand may rewrite a latched position's content"). The security page's good practice tells a person to "latch the whole block, not a part". The walk wrote the true behaviour into the schematic's 1.11 and 5.

**The question.** Is that the design, a part-latch being a part-latch and the root the way to hold the whole, or a hole?

**Recommended: bind them.** A whole-block write or a wipe of a block that holds any latch beneath an open root would need a key that proves every latch it would overwrite or erase. Otherwise it is refused, with the way through in the words: latch the root, or write the parts under their own keys. In practice that is the one holder who latched a part of their own block, and they pass. A block holding no latch at all stays free to any hand, so the open boards (`marks`, the pools, `presence`), their tidy, the door's last copy and the owner's set-aside are all unchanged. It closes the hole for exactly the blocks the walk found exposed: a roster whose members homesteaded their digits, a page latched at one entry. The cost: a squatter's latch at one position of an open block holds its whole-shape acts against the block's own author. That is visible, and answered by the author latching the root first, as the security page already tells them, or by the steward's set-aside. It changes the latch's rules in the kernel the wire is freezing, so it waits for this word, and lands with a line in the protocol's §2, the smoke the battery named ("a wipe of a block latched only at a position"), and the router's `smoke:locks` cases read against it.

**Answers.** GO: bind them as above. KEEP: the design stands, a part-latch being a part-latch. The schematic's 5 and 1.11 already say so, and the README would say it of every block, not only `settings`. CHANGE: say what.

### 5.2 The voice at an open beach: may it found a passport? (§8 item 12)

**What stands.** The voice founds a newcomer's mirrors at an open beach (`enter`, `button/voice.js:561`), each under a key made on the device, but never their passport. `wear` (`718`) needs one to stand, its `publish` (`703`) answering "no passport:<name> on this beach". `join` (`301`) needs an invitation, and an open beach mints none: `invite` (`276`) answers that anyone may come in. So a newcomer who enters through a page carrying the voice has a name the beach does not hold for them. They cannot `wear` their word, so they seal nothing and hold no colour. And the name is open to anyone else's passport. Once another hand founds `passport:<their name>` locked, every block the newcomer founds after under that name is refused `handle_bound`, since their key does not prove a passport someone else holds. Their mirrors already latched stay theirs. Other doors found a passport at an open beach (a `bsp()` write through the router, among them); the voice is the one that brings people in through pages and games.

**The question.** Should the voice found a passport at an open beach, and how?

**Recommended: one way in, at both kinds of beach.** `join({ name, passport })` with no invitation, at an open beach, founds `passport:<name>` locked under a key made on the device (or the person's own), says it once (`made()`), and proves the halves it derives (`wear`). It refuses a name that stands, as the handler already does (a standing block is never founded twice, and a latched one never without its key). At a members' beach `join` still needs the invitation. A page then offers "keep this name" at the end of a game, or at its first tap if the page prefers; `enter` stays as it is unless a page asks it to found the passport with the first mirror. The two then share one key, which is what the handle-bound rule expects. It is a change to the voice alone, the package's own.

**Answers.** GO: `join` founds at an open beach as above. GO WITH ENTER: `enter` also founds the passport with a newcomer's first mirror, unasked. KEEP: passports at an open beach come through the other doors, and the voice founds mirrors. CHANGE: say what.
