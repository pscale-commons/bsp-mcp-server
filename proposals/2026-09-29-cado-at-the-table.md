evening is the watcher's seat in the mirror (xstream-bsp #364 and its follow-up), which this

## 1. The law that already answers it

CADO faces are never stored: a face is **read off the hands — which member of a compound the
writing lands in**. That is the whole systemic answer, and everything below is its consequence:

| face | what they write | where it lands | who reads it there |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Character** | what the character does or says | the room's liquid, then its solid (`pool:<room>`) — the record | everyone at the table |
| **Observer** | what they see, ask, remark | their own mirror of the room at the room's address — `<room>:<handle>`, the family law's mirror, never the room's record | other observers, and the fold |
| **Author** | a place, a person, a thing of the world | their mirror of the world's register at the register's address — `spatial:<world>:<handle>`, `identity:<world>:<handle>` — adopted by the keeper into the register | the keeper, then everyone |
| **Designer** | a rule | their mirror of the rules — `rules:<world>:<handle>` — adopted into `rules:<world>` | the keeper, then the room's law |

One mirror, one box. What changes is not the surface but **the block the line goes to**, and the
block is the face. The mirror's context word already cycles *table · the mirror · my agent*: the
table word is the Character's block; the same word, cycled further, is *watch* (the observer's
mirror of the room), *author* (the register), *design* (the rules). The line goes where the word
says; the record stays the Character's alone. This is the tree family — spine and mirrors at the
self-same addresses, folded by anyone — applied to a table, and it is why the mirror's V·L·S is not
too small: liquid is per address and per author already; a face only chooses the address family.

So the answers, one by one.

## 2. The Observer sees the resolution

Built this evening: a hand with no character at a table is a **watcher** — the room's record stands
in front of them, live, third person as it landed; the glass stays down; their line goes to the
mirror privately and never into the room; no telling is bought on their key, because the record IS
the resolution and a telling is a character's own story from their seat. That is the Observer's
first rung: **output only** ("forming is shared with those who share your absence"), which is
exactly what David asked for — *"I'd like to see third-party content — the resolution."*

**Anchored or locked?** Neither by force. An observer stands in a room by choice and moves freely
between rooms (the one-place law binds a character, not a watcher); they follow a character by the
ring on the site (the character's page, their pictures) and, in the mirror, by opening the room the
character stands in. A "follow Ugarth" that moves the watcher's view when Ugarth moves is one line
in the mirror — the character's room is already resolved every thirty seconds for the character's
own pin — and is named here, not built.

## 3. Observers talk among themselves — the watch mirror

David: *"observers could chat amongst themselves with their VLS — though I am not sure what the
Solid would be or where it would go."*

The solid is **each observer's own mirror of the room**: `<room>:<handle>` at the table's beach —
`130:happyseaurchin` — voiced at the room's own addresses (the beat the remark is about, as its
digit), exactly as a hand's mirror stands beside a spine. Vapour and liquid are the same as at any
address (typing seen by the co-present, one revisable slip per author); the fold across the
observers' mirrors is `<room>:watch` — anyone folds it, nobody owns it. Nothing of this touches
`pool:<room>`. The players never see it unless they open it (the drawer); the observers see each
other's; a picture-taker's studio (`ways:stills` 6.4) is the same shape with a picture as its
output. The "observer list" David wants limited is the limit a room's convention sets — a
`convention:<room>` dial for who may voice a watch mirror there — a declaration, not a wall.

**Where it feeds**: the semantic-flow visualiser reads mirrors at addresses; the watch fold is one
more mirror family at the room's addresses and rides in unchanged.

## 4. The Author changes the world — from the register, never the record

David: *"What if there are authors watching and they want to influence things, change the
characters or the scenery or settings?"*

An author does not write into the room. The world's things stand in the registers — places in
`spatial:<world>`, people and roles in `identity:<world>`, the reasons in `keeper:<world>` — and
the room is composed from them at every beat (the place line via the keeper's placing; the faces;
the look). So an author's act is a **proposal at the register's address**: their mirror
`spatial:<world>:<handle>` voiced at the address of the place they would change, or a new place
beneath it; `identity:<world>:<handle>` for a person. The keeper adopts by copying into the register
(the author's copy is the one copy — the same law as `gallery:<world>`), and the next beat composes
from the adopted line. The author never touches a beat that has landed; they change what the next
beat is made from. This is track B's parity table already: place and identity as addresses on the
world's own registers, a hand's mirror at those addresses.

**The /walk form**: yes — the author's door is a page, not the glass. `/walk/<world>` with the
spatial register as its ladder, each rung showing the register's line and the author's own mirror
line beside it, with one act: *propose*. Forms (#446, §8) is the lane that builds a family's form;
the author's walk is that form for a world's registers. The mirror stays clean.

## 5. The Designer changes the rules

The rules are a block the room's law reads — NOMAD at `rules:nomad`, swappable by the world
(`rules:<world>`). A designer's act is the same shape as the author's: a mirror `rules:<world>:<handle>`
at the rule's address, adopted by the keeper. Between adoption and the next beat nothing changes;
after it, every commit at the table resolves under the new line. No UI beyond the /walk form with
the rules block as the ladder.

## 6. Track A or track B?

Both, and they are not rivals here. **Track A** (the pool at a table in the mirror) is where the
Character plays and the Observer watches — the beat, the dice, the fold, the record; nothing in
this proposal changes its five pieces. **Track B** (the RPG on the clock — characters as users of
the world beach, place and time as addresses on the registers) is the shape the Author and Designer
already work in: their acts are register addresses, and this proposal takes exactly that from B
without running B's clock. If B is ever run, the observers' watch mirrors and the authors' register
mirrors are already B's organs.

## 7. What is built, what is one rung, what is a lane

- **Built**: the watcher (Observer, output only) — #364 and the follow-up.
- **One rung each** (small, in the mirror): *follow a character*; the context word cycling to
  *watch* with the watch mirror as its block; the observer's fold behind the situation's drawer.
- **A lane each**: the author's /walk form over the registers with *propose* and the keeper's
  *adopt* (rides forms #446); the designer's rules form (the same form, another ladder); the studio
  for pictures (`ways:stills` 6.4) as the watch mirror's picture-shaped sibling.
- **Not needed**: a stored face, an observer role, an author role; a second surface; a change to
  the beach.

## 8. For David's ruling

1. The face is the block the line lands in — the table for the character, a mirror at the room's
   address for the observer, a mirror at the register's address for the author and designer. Yes or
   no to this as the law.
2. Observers' solid: `<room>:<handle>` mirrors at the room's addresses, folded at `<room>:watch`, a
   room's convention dial limiting who voices. Yes or no.
3. The author's and designer's door: the /walk form over the registers, *propose* and *adopt*, in
   the forms lane. Yes or no.
4. Order: the observer's rungs first (follow, the watch word), then the author's form, then the
   designer's, then the studio.
