# The studio on the clock — an observer team that lands (2026-10-08)

**Status**: PROPOSED at David's ask (8 October: *"take a step back and process this please, and propose
recommendations on how we can move forwards with this structurally"*). Nothing built, nothing written on

## 0. The answer in one paragraph

An observer team is a **family on the table's clock**. Each observer says into their own mirror at the beat
they are looking at. The one who lands — the lander — folds the team's lines, under the studio's law, into
the shot: the fold IS the compiled prompt, the picture is an entry in the lander's own book addressed to that
beat, and the film is the lander's tree read in time order. The team is whoever the lander's law names:
anyone may mirror, only the named are folded. Keys never leave the lander's vault; the law names the maker
and the form, and the beach composes the call every seat runs. Nothing is new in the substrate — a spine
(the clock), mirrors, a fold under a function, the edit-latch on the tree. The studio law that stands today
(`function:studio`, a pool with two shutters) becomes this.

## 2. Mark's shared folders, and the lander's library

David is right, and the law already says it. Continuity is held by pictures (6.5): the newest picture of
the same place or person rides as a reference, found by address in the maker's book. A team shares a library
by sharing a lander, not a folder: the lander's book is the continuity. A second lander is a second
continuity over the same play, and nothing stops two teams working one table. No folder, no copy.

## 3. The shape: a studio is a family

| | the block | who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| the spine | the table's clock — `spine:<studio>` founded on the clock as `/found` founds a venture; no address is ever typed, *"a time names its beat"* | the clock |
| a mirror | `<studio>:<observer>` — the observer's line at the beat they comment on | each observer, under their own key |
| the tree | `<studio>` — the fold at the beat: the shot as landed, and the link to the picture | the lander, under their key (the latch is the leadership) |
| the law | `function:<studio>` | the lander |
| the pictures | `gallery:<lander>`, each entry addressed to the beat | the lander's seat, on the lander's key |

`function:<studio>` says, in its branches: **1** what a fold makes — the forms, one per branch (1.1 a
still, 1.2 a clip from it, 1.3 a character's own perspective); **2** whose mirrors fold — the team, by
handle; **3** the maker — the service and model, what it accepts as references, and that references are
the lander's own book; **4** where pictures land; **5** who lands — the lander, or *first fold stands*
where the tree is left open (the clock table's rule, #496); **6** the recipe it mounts, `ways:stills` 6, by
reference; **7** the landing law the mirror already reads — collective by default, PERSONAL for a member's
own cut.

A studio over a table played in rooms (track A) addresses the same way: an observer's line names the
moment it is about, and the moment's own time is its beat.

## 4. Issue 1 — how the lander folds only the voices they want

The fold list is the curation: `function:<studio>` 2 names the handles whose mirrors fold. A studio is its
own family, named by its lander, so a thousand observers at a table are a thousand mirrors in their own
families, not in this one. Anyone may say into a mirror of this family — the beach is open — and a stranger's
line stands visible beside the team's; it is not folded. Joining is founding your mirror and a lists line,
as every family; being folded is being named. If strangers' lines ever crowd the snapshot, the router
reads the law's list when it lays the mirrors side by side — one rung, when it is needed.

Teams of teams are trees over trees: a league's mirrors are the studios' trees. Identity is who speaks (the
handle on the mirror) and who lands (the latch on the tree), never an address; mirrors are blocks, not
digits, so nine does not bind a team.

## 5. Issue 2 — the API, and the function that delivers the synthesis

The law names the maker and the form; the key stays in the lander's vault and the seat takes it from there
(`ways:vault`). The beach composes **the shot** as it composes the three tiers of play — the law
(`function:<studio>` with `ways:stills` 6 mounted), the bundle at the beat (the table's own fold or telling,
the place, the principals under that address, the cast's looks and faces, the team's lines at the beat,
the look), and a contract (return the prompt, the references by address, the form) — so every seat runs the
same synthesis: the mirror's camera, a page, a person's own LLM, Limner. The seat runs it on its own LLM
key, sends the prompt to the maker on its image key, and lands the link. Different forms within one studio
are branches of its law; a different synthesis is a different studio.

## 6. Issue 3 — what an observer sees, and where they speak

The observer's view is a filter over the table's clock, and the filter is theirs on the beach:
`lists:<handle>` already holds the places a hand has open (branch 3, the mirror's row); the same block
holds which characters they follow, which observers they are with, and which solid they see — the
resolutions, a character's tellings, the studio's folds and pictures. The mirror reads it and draws one
stream, by beat, not by room; the characters' lines in grey, the studio's in its own colour. Their input is a
say into their studio mirror at the beat in view; a tap on a solid line puts its address into the say, so a
comment is about something. The pictures stand beneath the beat they are addressed to. *Make it happen* is the
fold for the lander and nothing for anyone else. The mirror's own voice and its glass are unchanged.

An author's view is the same filter over the registers' mirrors (CADO §4): places and characters as the
chips, the fold into the register as the landing.

The mirror already opens a family by its field and offers say, land it and make it happen at a beat, so a
studio family can be worked in it today. What is to build there is the chips and the camera.
