## 1. What two players met on 5 October (`/w/clock-two-walk`)

Two characters under two keys, the mirror's own sequence of router calls, Sonnet 5 on the rig key.
What worked: both said at a beat and read each other's line; the first presser's fold, lean, telling
and move; the second player's telling and move through the router's door. What broke:

- **Only the first presser could ever make it happen.** The night was born locked under the first
  folder's key; the second player's press spent a model call and was refused.
- **The world spoke for the players' characters.** The lean wrote `VOICE hobb` and `VOICE wenna`
  (two passes of two), seating lowercase twins of both characters under the folder's key; the next
  fold wove those lines as deeds and speech their players never gave, and the door told Wenna her
  line already stood.
- **One character's going moved both.** The fold's single bare `WAY` moved every passport told by it
  — and to 200, an ancestor address no way offered.
- **A companion was named unheard**: fold and tellings used the handles though no name had been
  said aloud.
- **In the mirror only the presser was told and moved on** (read in `Mirror.tsx`, not walked there).

## 2. The ruling it follows, and the question it answers

David, 2026-10-03 (the author's keeper, §2): *make-it-happen and the telling stay the player's own,
on their key; the world's next move is the keeper's.* A night locked under one player's key
contradicts the first half, so `task:weft` 21 — a shared table key, or a doorman as the keeper's
hand? — is answered by neither: **where no keeper's hand is named at `keeper:scene` 4 the table is
its players'**: any seated character's hand folds, the night is born open, and the first fold at an
address stands. Where a hand IS named, the night is born locked under its key and the characters
say and wait, as the door already said.
