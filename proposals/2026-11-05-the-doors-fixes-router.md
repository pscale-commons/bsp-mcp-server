## 6. Sign-in at a members' beach: the shape, for David's word

**What stands.** The ring of fingerprints lives at `DEFAULT_BEACH`, and a fingerprint is minted only for a handle whose passport stands there (the key-in-the-connector record, §9 and §10; #597). Through the hosted router, whose beach is the apex, a member who stands only at SALT cannot sign in; and a router whose own beach is a members' beach signs nobody in.

**Why the page asking for the beach is not enough.** Minting could run at the door, with the typed handle and key proving a pass at the member's beach. But every call after reads the ring at that beach to open the key, and a members' beach shows a router nothing without a pass, which only the key proves, and the key is inside the ring. Holding a pass from the sign-in lasts twelve hours and dies with every redeploy, so a connection would sign in and then stop working.

**The shape that would work**, each step a design change:

1. The sign-in page asks for the member's beach beside the handle, the apex by default; the token carries that beach, sealed as the fingerprint already is.
2. Minting runs at the door: the handle and the typed key prove a pass at that beach, the passport is read there, and the ring is founded there under the key.
3. The ring at a members' beach is served to anyone, so the router reads it keyless on every call: it is sealed by construction, holding nothing a reader can open, and the apex already shows every ring. Two ways to say so:
   - the beach package's gate serves `fingerprints:<handle>` to a stranger as it serves what a holder marks beyond (its `beyondOf`, `api/pscale-beach.js`); or
   - minting marks the ring beyond in the member's own index. But a member with no index of their own would be given one, and the gate then reads that index for every block of theirs, so blocks the keepers had marked beyond would go dark. So this way would write only into an index that already stands.
4. Each later call opens the key from the ring and proves the member's pass with it, as signed calls do now.

The first way is a few lines in pscale-beach's gate and a few here (the page's field, the token's beach, minting at the door, the ring read at the token's beach). Both change what a members' beach shows a stranger (that a ring exists, and its sealed bytes), so the choice is David's.

## 7. What was left, and why

- **Sign-in at a members' beach**: §6, for David's word.
- **`bsp()`'s own refusals stay ordinary results.** The door's refusal and the genus guard's are errors now, as the router's thrown errors and the sign-in wrapper's refusals were. bsp()'s "Write rejected" lines, a wrong key among them, still come back as results; turning every one into an error changes what every client reads, and §8 did not name it.
- **`pscale_genus` does not stand at the door.** Its store posts for itself, so it carries no pass and wakes nobody behind a wall. Not in §8; noted for whoever takes the genus door next.
- **Two concurrent first proofs can race.** At a 401 the door forgets the session's pass before proving, so a call can drop the pass a concurrent call has just minted. The play door's reads that prove run one after another, so this walk never met it; left as seen.
- **The self line's other salt.** The walk found a frame-sealed self line opening at the router by bare handle (1.41). Only the trailing slash was named; the bare-handle divide stands as drawn.
