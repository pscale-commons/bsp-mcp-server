## 0. The fault in the mirror as it stands

The mirror answers two questions well and one not at all.

- **Where am I?** The tabs: the beach, a table, a room. Good.
- **To whom does this line go?** The context word beside the box: the table, the mirror, my agent.
  Good.
- **Which layer am I looking at, and which layer does my line affect?** Not answered. The mirror
  derives it from the handle: a handle at a table IS a character, and everything on screen is the
  Character's — the narrative, the staged lines, the vapour. An observer had nowhere to stand, so
  a "watcher" was bolted on beside the table and their view was moved behind the glass. That is the
  confusion David named: the glass is for engaging the mirror, not for looking at the play.

The systemic answer: make the layer explicit, put it in the frame, and keep the glass a glass.

## 1. Three words in the frame's head

Every screen of the mirror answers three questions in one line, in this order:

```
brackenfoot · 130 · The Long House      as Ugarth ▾        to: the table ▾
   WHERE (the tabs, as now)              WHICH LAYER        TO WHOM (as now)
```

- **Where** is the place stood at — a room at a table, the beach, a world. Unchanged. For an
  observer it may also be a character followed (§4).
- **Which layer** is the face, and it is the new word. It cycles through the faces the hand can
  take here: *as Ugarth* (Character), *watching* (Observer), *authoring* (Author), *designing*
  (Designer). Only the faces the hand can take are offered: Character when the handle has a
  passport at this table; Observer always; Author and Designer when the hand holds the world's key
  (or, later, as proposals to the keeper). The default is Character if you have one here, else
  Observer. The layer word tints the whole frame, so what you are looking at is never in doubt.
- **To whom** cycles as now — the layer (the table, the studio, the register, the rules), the
  mirror (private), my agent. The act word follows the pair: *say · make it happen* at the table,
  *say · land* in the studio, *propose* at a register, *ask* to the mirror, *wake* to the agent.

One box. Cycling the layer changes what the frame shows and what the box writes into. Cycling
"to" changes whether the line goes to the layer or to the voice. The glass is only ever the voice.

## 2. Each layer: what it shows, what a line affects, where it lands

A layer is a **spine and the hands' lines at its addresses** — the tree family drawn on the glass.
One renderer serves all four: the layer's record at this place, and beneath each rung the lines the
hands of that layer have put there. Only the blocks differ.

### Character — *as Ugarth*
- **Shows**: the narrative — the room's record as it landed, or the character's own story when the
  hand is keyed; the others' staged lines and vapour; the situation (place, who is here, the ways)
  in its drawer. Exactly the table as it is today.
- **A line affects**: the room. Say stages it in the room's liquid; make it happen commits the
  beat under the room's law.
- **Lands in**: `pool:<room>`, its liquid. Unchanged.

### Observer — *watching*
- **Shows**: the narrative, plain and live, read-only (the spine); and threaded beside the beats,
  the observers' own lines about them; at the foot, the observers' staged lines and vapour. The
  observers see each other here; the players never see this unless they open the drawer.
- **A line affects**: the studio — the observers' room beside the table's room, the room
  `ways:stills` 5 and 6.4 already name. It is a pool like any other, so the mirror's staging,
  vapour and landing serve it unchanged; it declares itself a studio (no doorman, no dice), and a
  line may carry the beat it answers so it is shown beside that beat.
- **Lands in**: `pool:<room>-studio`. Never `pool:<room>`.
- **Buys**: nothing. The narrative is the resolution as it landed; no telling is composed on an
  observer's key. A fold of the studio, if ever wanted, is the reader's own soft call.
- **The limit David asked for** — who may voice the studio — is one dial in the studio's own
  convention block, set by the table's host: open, the table's players and named hands, or named
  hands only. A declaration, not a wall.

### Author — *authoring*
- **Shows**: the world's content at this place — the place line from the spatial register, its
  parent, the people set here from the identity register, the keeper's reason where one stands;
  beneath each, the authors' proposals for it.
- **A line affects**: the registers, by proposal. The author never touches a beat that has
  landed; they change what the next beat is composed from. The keeper adopts, and the next beat
  composes from the adopted line.
- **Lands in**: `spatial:<world>:<handle>`, `identity:<world>:<handle>` — the author's mirror at
  the register's own address; adoption is the keeper's copy into the register.
- **Its own page**: the walk form over the registers (`/walk/<world>`, the forms lane) is the same
  layer as a page, for authoring at length; the mirror's author layer is the same acts at the
  place stood in.

### Designer — *designing*
- **Shows**: the rules in force here (`rules:<world>`, NOMAD by default), each rung with the
  designers' proposals beneath.
- **A line affects**: the rules, by proposal; adoption by the keeper; every commit after it
  resolves under the new line.
- **Lands in**: `rules:<world>:<handle>`.

## 3. The glass stays the voice

The frosted glass is engagement with the mirror and nothing else: a private question answered
from where the hand stands, in the layer it stands in — *what do I see?* as Ugarth, *who is
speaking?* as an observer, *what does the register say here?* as an author. It never carries a
layer's record. When the glass is up, the layer stands behind it, frosted; when it comes down, the
layer is there. Nothing said to the mirror rides vapour or lands anywhere.

## 4. Where an observer stands

An observer stands in a room by choice and moves freely — the one-place law binds a character,
never a watcher. Two ways to choose:

- **A room**: the tab, as now.
- **A character**: *following Ugarth* — a chip in the tab row that moves when Ugarth moves. The
  character's room is already resolved every thirty seconds for the character's own pin; following
  is that resolution applied to someone else's handle. The observer then stands in Ugarth's room
  and in that room's studio.

An observer is therefore anchored to nothing and locked to nothing. They are where they chose to
be, and the studio they write in is the studio of the room they are in.

## 5. The frame, drawn

The Character's frame is the mirror as it is today. The Observer's frame, at the same room:

```
┌ brackenfoot · 130 · The Long House    watching ▾    to: the studio ▾ ┐
│                                                                        │
│  Ugarth plants his feet in the small room and lets his voice…          │  ← the narrative
│     ⌐ happyseaurchin: he's overplayed his hand with Vane                │    (read-only)
│     ⌐ marram: the factor never looked up — watch that ledger           │  ← observers' lines
│                                                                        │    at this beat
│  The factor's fingertip stays where it landed on the ledger…           │
│                                                                        │
│  "An appraisal." Reeve Haldan's voice precedes him into the doorway…   │
│     ⌐ happyseaurchin: here comes the reeve                              │
│                                                                        │
│  ── the studio ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────  │
│  marram is typing…                                          (vapour)   │
│  happyseaurchin · staged: "Haldan will set a condition"  [land]        │  ← the observers'
│                                                                        │    liquid
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  say something to the other watchers…                        [say]     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The tint is the observer's; the players' narrative is plain; the observers' lines are indented
under the beat they answer. Tap *watching ▾* and it becomes *as Ugarth ▾* (if you hold Ugarth
here): the observers' lines fall away, the players' staged lines and vapour take the foot, and the
act becomes *say · make it happen*. Tap *to: the studio ▾* and it becomes *to: the mirror*: the
glass comes up over exactly this frame, and the act becomes *ask*.

The Author's frame at the same room shows the Long House as the register has it, with proposals
beneath; the Designer's, the rules. Same shape, other blocks.

## 6. Identity, and the default face

The face is not the handle. happyseaurchin is an Observer at Ugarth's table and an Author of
brackenfoot when he brings the world's key; Ugarth is a Character. The identity card is unchanged:
one handle, one key. What the hand may be here is read off what it holds here — a passport at this
table, the world's key — and the default face follows: Character if you have one here, else
Observer. A person with two handles in two tabs (one identity per tab, built) is a Character in
one and an Observer in the other without ceremony.

## 7. What exists to build on — this is not a rewrite

- The tabs: where. Unchanged.
- The context word and the act: to whom. Gains a sibling, the layer word.
- The record renderer, the social surface (vapour, staged lines), the seen-marker, the polling: all
  keyed on a pool name. The observer layer points them at the studio pool.
- The situation strip and its drawer (place, ways, cast, the record): the author layer reads the
  same registers it already reads, and shows the proposals beneath.
- The glass and the identity card: unchanged.
- The watcher's seat (built): becomes the Observer layer's condition — no passport here — and its
  guards stay (nothing an observer writes reaches the table).
- The studio: named in `ways:stills`; a pool with a declared convention; the same machinery.

## 8. The order, when ruled

1. The layer word, with Character and Observer only; the studio pool beside each table room; the
   observers' lines threaded under the beats. This alone makes the mirror operable for both faces.
2. Following a character.
3. The Author layer over the registers, with propose and adopt; the walk form beside it.
4. The Designer layer over the rules.
