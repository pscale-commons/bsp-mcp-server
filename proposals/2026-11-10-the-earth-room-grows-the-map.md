## 1. Two acts, kept apart

**Standing somewhere** stays what it was: one line in a person's own passport, under their own key (`function:earth` 7, char-creation 0).

**Growing the map** is new, and it is not tied to a passport. Anyone may propose a place the map lacks. A proposal places nobody. It only asks the map to grow, so it needs no holder and no key.

## 2. Why no doorman

A doorman would only be needed to write the map *live*. That means an agent awake during the talk holding the key the map answers to, and the standing rule says an LLM may point at the door and never carry the key (watch:weft 219).

David's design needs nothing written live under a key:

- **The proposal** is an open write to the room.
- **The commitment** is the keeper's evening pass. It already holds the key on the Mac (`wake:weft` 6.7, `function:earth` 7).

## 4. The room, and what the pass does with it

`pool:earth` voices take the ordinary shape: `{_: line, 1: who, 2: address, 3: time, 4: face}`. The globe writes the line in person-words with the anchor in geo:earth's own form, for example:

`Leeds — a place the map lacks, proposed from the globe at 53.8,-1.54 · 53.64,53.96,-1.7,-1.38; it falls in United Kingdom (spatial:earth:31100000000).`

The remaining positions:
- `1` is `globe`, the door it came through.
- `2` is the containing ground's address, full width.
- `4` is `author`, because growing a world is an author's act.

The pass, at 7.1 as amended:

- **Reads** every line not yet answered, as a stated place for 7.2.
- **Checks it against the world** before minting: is it real, what rung its population sets, where in the map it belongs.
- **Answers beneath the line**, in the room: the address it now has, or why it was not added. Never in a parlour, since a proposal has no holder.

An answered line, or a place the map now holds by that name nearby, leaves the globe's dashed pins.

## 5. Privacy

- **Never finer than a town.** A street, a house or a shop typed into the search is narrowed to the town that holds it. A house or a room is a person's own to name, in their own passport or nowhere.
- **The point is rounded to two decimals**, about a kilometre.
- **"My location"** is worked out on the device, walked down geo:earth's anchors, and sent nowhere unless the person proposes.
- **Typed words** go to OpenStreetMap's search (Nominatim), once, on Enter, within its usage policy. The globe credits it.
