# Where is "here" — canon, play, and the person; and what the three phases mean at scale

**Date**: 2026-08-08
**Status**: PROPOSAL — nothing moved. Written before any write, per grips:3 and the
law-write discipline (2026-08-05).
**Occasion**: David's question — "when I engage Julie in a pool that is not a fantasy
location, where exactly should we specify where we are meeting?" — and, the same hour,
an hour lost to a pool named `dovetail` existing twice.

---

## 1. Why this is law-class and not a preference

Three things collided on 2026-08-08 and each was correct alone:

- a room name resolves against whatever beach a column holds, and the beach is sticky;
- a beach seeds a `_` on any block born from a first write, so nothing is floor-0;
- the client renders a pool's `_` as its welcome.

The result was one name, two blocks, and no way to see which you stood in — for the
beach's own steward, in a room he had made, with a friend invited into it. The client
fixes for that shipped the same day (xstream-bsp #222). **This proposal is about the
question underneath, which code did not answer: what does a location MEAN on this
substrate, and which of the several senses of "here" is the one a person is in.**

It is law-class because the answer decides whether `earth.beach` keeps its own
passports, and that is the world-genome's territory (the scenario·table law, PR #227).

## 3. The three layers, which the substrate already practises

The fantasy side is not an open question; it is an unwritten answer. Naming it:

| layer | lives at | holds | may an instance live here? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **canon** | `<world>.beach` | `spatial:` `temporal:` `identity:` `keeper:` | **no** |
| **play** | apex bubbles, `/w/<table>` | scenes, rosters, residue of what happened | yes — and it CITES canon |
| **the agent** | the apex | `shell:` `passport:` `history:` grains | yes — one per handle, ever |

Read down the Farbur/Ubarak pair and the rule falls out: **a canon figure is a name in a
register; an operating agent is a shell at the apex; a table holds only what happened at
that table.** Farbur is not in urb because instances are not canon. Ubarak is in urb
because he *is* canon, and his apex shell is a second, different fact about him — the
agency, not the figure.

**Proposed as law**: canon holds no instances. A world's beach carries registers only.
Anything that plays, cites.

## 4. Bubble → canon is liquid → solid, one scale up

David's reading, and it is right: what happens in a bubble may be "upgraded" to canon
much as liquid is upgraded to solid.

The mechanism already exists and needs no invention — **proposal-by-mirror** (`tree:5.1`):
voice the change at the canon address in your OWN block, the fold surfaces it, adoption
is the holder's, and `archive:<block>:<date>` is the version record. That is precisely
liquid→solid at the scale of a world: staged in a mirror anyone may write, committed by
the keeper alone.

**Proposed as law**: canon changes by adoption from a mirror, never by direct edit under
a live table. The keeper's register is the solid; every player's mirror is the liquid.

## 5. The real-world side: mirrors, not second passports

`spatial:earth` is a spine. The tree family (`tree:8`) already says what sits on one:
`spine:V` the un-owned coordinate space, `V:<handle>` the sovereign mirrors at the
self-same addresses, `V` the computed fold, `pool:V` the gathering.

So the per-person blocks in `earth.beach` want to be **mirrors**, not passports:

- a **passport is identity** — ONE per handle, at the apex, beside the shell and the
  grains that make it reachable;
- a **mirror is position on a spine** — many per handle, one per spine you appear on,
  named `earth:<handle>`.

This gets exactly what the earth work is for — a correct map of real people — without a
second identity for the same person. `earth.beach` is currently *shaped* like a world
(own passports, own char-creation) and *named* like a lens. It should pick.

**Proposed**: earth is a LENS. Its per-person blocks become `earth:<handle>` mirrors on
`spatial:earth`; identity stays singular at the apex. Migration is a copy-then-verify,
then a delete, with `archive:` records — no data loss, and it can wait for David's word.

**The general rule this instances**: a world that models the real is a lens and takes
mirrors; a world that models a fiction is a canon and takes characters. The difference is
not the content, it is whether the people in it are the same people.

## 6. Rooms fall out of an address space — and the space need not be a spine

The question "where do Julie and I meet" has a better answer than a new block.

`pscale_pool_engage at=` narrows by digit-prefix. **One located pool therefore serves
every node of its address space**, and a shared room is COMPUTED, not created: the room
David and Matthew share is simply the longest common prefix of their two addresses. One
does not author an England pool; England *is* the prefix where Birmingham and
Northampton meet.

And the address space need not be a `spine:`. **`at=` does not care what kind of block
the address belongs to — a pscale address is a pscale address.** So a `sed:` collective
works identically: `at=1` the roster, `at=1.1` a member's position, `at=2.1` a project.
Matthew's `sed:LERO NNH` already has exactly that shape — members at 1.1 and 1.2, the
Recovery Festival at 2.1 — and a room falls out of every one of them with no new
machinery.

This answers David's later question directly: **sed is not an alternative base to spine;
they are two kinds of address space, and rooms fall out of either.** What differs is what
the addresses MEAN and who owns them:

| | address means | ownership | best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **spine + mirrors** | a dimension everyone speaks to | each person owns their whole mirror block | structure to coordinate around |
| **sed** | order of arrival | one block, per-position locks | a roster, provably, in order |
| **grain** | a met pair | both sides, symmetric | the edges — who actually knows whom |

A community wants all three: a roster to say who is in, grains to say who has met, and a
spine only once there is structure worth coordinating around. **The sed is the more
natural base for a community that exists before it has structure** — which is what LERO
NNH is, and why Matthew reached for it unprompted.

**Proposed as convention, not law**: a room is located against whatever address space the
group already keeps. Do not mint a spine to justify a room.

## 7. The phases have different pscale ceilings

David: "pools at high pscale can't work like normal pools … there could be millions of
people engaging at the england pscale room."

Correct, and the reason is sharper than volume:

- **Vapour is floor-only.** Live presence exists at pscale 0, now. There is no vapour at
  England scale — not because too many people would be in it, but because *simultaneity
  is not defined there*. A place that large has no "now" that its inhabitants share.
- **Liquid scales.** Staged intent at a coarse address is a STANDING intention, legible
  over months without co-presence. This is the register that makes a coarse room useful.
- **Solid scales indefinitely.** A record is a record at any grain.

And the coupling David named: **place +N implies beat +N.** Room/now, city/season,
England/decade. This extends the S·T ladder already ruled core (beat negotiation:
"lowest active pscale drives; the authored floor is the anti-bullet-time") with the rule
that **the place's pscale sets the floor for the beat**, not only the participants' rates.

It also explains the RPG parking David described: coarse placement is not a room you
stand in, it is a rung you WAIT on — and the wait is a grain, not a stall.

**Proposed as law**: vapour is floor-bound; liquid and solid are not. A client must not
offer live presence at a coarse address, and should say why rather than showing an empty
room.

## 8. What is NOT in this proposal — buildable now, no decision needed

Recorded here so they are not lost, and so the proposal is not held hostage to them:

1. **Vapour scoped by grain.** At any address, show vapour only from handles the reader
   holds a grain with, optionally one hop out (the organic/organic+1 shape in the
   open-commons posture). This makes coarse-scale presence tractable by REACH rather than
   by ranking or moderation — no feed, no cap, no algorithm. It is the best of the ideas
   here and it needs no law: grains already are the awareness graph. **Design first, per
   the standing rule for anything SAND-adjacent.**
2. **An apex entry in the `worlds` register** — `commons → https://beach.happyseaurchin.com`
   — so links to the main beach read `?world=commons&room=x` instead of a percent-encoded
   URL inside a URL. One block write, no code.
3. **A bare `?room=` resetting to the default beach**, making the location param
   unnecessary in the common case. Small PR. Changes behaviour for anyone sitting in a
   world who clicks a bare room link, so it wants David's word rather than a default.

## 9. What this proposal asks for

One decision, and it is item 5: **is `earth` a lens on real people, or a world with its
own inhabitants?** Everything else here either records what the substrate already does
(3, 4, 6) or states a rule that no existing block contradicts (7).

If it is a lens: the earth per-person blocks become mirrors, identity stays singular at
the apex, and the general rule at the end of §5 goes into the world-genome as the test
for any future world.

If it is a world: then the duplicate passports are correct, and what needs writing
instead is the rule that a handle's apex identity and its earth identity are *different
entities that share a name* — which is exactly the confusion that cost an hour at the
scale of a room, so it should be adopted only deliberately.

Nothing moves until David says which.
