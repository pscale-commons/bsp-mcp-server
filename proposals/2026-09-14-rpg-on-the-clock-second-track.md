## 0. The non-multiplication commitment

Zero new primitives, zero new block families, zero daemons, zero stored clocks. Everything
below is the S·T·I registers the world already has, mirrors at their own addresses (the
family law), one operator block, and the doorman as it stands with two extensions already

tried beside the running one and jettisoned clean (the 2026-08-06 law).

group, the room a pool at the arrival address, GRIT mounted at its underscore, the window

The two run in parallel because B changes the flow, not the pool: the sealed trial of
August ruled acts to the pool for the SAME flow, and B is a different flow. Its verdict is
not overturned by this proposal; it is re-asked for a new shape, by the same method.

## 3. Time — the one asymmetry, David's, and the core of the track

On earth the now is set at the living rate: one computed address for everyone, the past
lit, the future dark. Intentions ahead are voiced at their addresses and the fold meets
them when now catches up (the capstone's second use-case). Nobody advances the clock.

In the fantasy the now is PLAYED. A party stands at a beat of the world's clock and
advances it by folding: the fold at a beat is the beat's end, and the party's temporal
standpoint moves to the next. Moving up a rung is zoom-out and fast-forward in one act
(grit 1.52, 1.55): the settling beat is committed, the standpoint coarsens to the day, and
the fold at the day contains the beats beneath it. Different parties stand at different
addresses of one clock; the world's now is not a number but a set of lit addresses. The
world's clock is a block universe entered at any address, and the keeper's lean — the
Coming Periods, `temporal:urb` 7 — is the authored trajectory ahead of now, which the
capstone names the sign of the time ahead. The now-advance dial (capstone §4) is the
per-world declaration that already distinguishes the two: `rules:earth` 6 wall-coupled,
`rules:thornwood` 4 attention-elastic. B makes the dial the whole of the difference.

**The table dissolves into time.** Hundreds of groups play Brackenfoot at hundreds of
addresses; replay is a later address; the record at (place, beat) never collides. Two
groups at one place and one hour meet, because the world is one — the open shared world
David asked for in the scope, arrived at not by instancing but by time.

**Where the standpoint lives.** `passport:<handle>` position 3 carries the place today, as
a star-ref into the scenario's own spatial block. In B it carries the beat too — a second
star-ref beside the first, into `temporal:urb` — and a move in time is the same four-step
move as a move in space, with a temporal address.

**The world's ladder must be declared first.** `temporal:urb` is poured as seven epochs,
coarse over fine, not the Gregorian sundial: the fantasy needs its own rungs beneath the
epoch (reign, year, season, month, week, day, gathering, beat) and its floor fixed, in the
place the sundial names for it — the world's rules block (sundial 7). That declaration is
an Author's write in David's world and the first act of any trial.

## 5. The hard problem — one night is one night

The pool gives two things the stream refuses by design: an irrevocable beat and one atomic
resolution per window. B must get "one night" from a ROLE rather than the store:

- THE DETERMINER is the keeper's seat — a person wearing `keeper:urb`, or the doorman
  standing in for it. Its kept fold at the beat address is the night. Absent a keeper at
  the beat, the party's earliest hand folds, as at the pool, and the keeper may later
  ratify or rule (grit 8.5). Supersession is the keeper's alone, and a superseding fold
  says what it supersedes. This is an operator law in `function:urb`, not code.
- A CHARACTER'S LINE stays sovereign and revisable in its own mirror; the fold names what
  it folded, with the stamps it read, so a line revised after the fold is visibly later
  than the night it was folded into. Two accounts, as the subjective design always had:
  the character's, and the world's.
- A CONTEST is two lines at one beat that decide one will (grit 1.62); the determiner
  weaves them with each actor's dice; a simple act never rolls.

The three failure modes the trial watches for: rival nights at one beat; a revised line
after the fold read as if it preceded it; a party advancing past a beat another party
still stands in at the same place (the capstone's coarse-resolution gates decide who
waits).

## 7. The sealed trial

Rung 1 per the 2026-08-06 method: semantic, no client, no deploy, one afternoon, run by a
fresh session with two blind character seats, one keeper seat, and one observer judge who
reads both arms without knowing which is which.

**The arms.** P: the pool at `/w/brackenfoot-open` as it runs. S: the same scenario on the
clock at `urb.beach` — the characters' passports at urb with the place a star-ref into
brackenfoot's spatial block and the beat a star-ref into `temporal:urb`; their lines at
`temporal:<handle>`; the keeper's folds kept PERSONALLY at `tree:temporal:urb:<keeper>`,
so the world's own chronicle is not written by a trial. The scenario stays canon.

**The seal — the questions, fixed here before any run.**
1. ONE NIGHT: after six beats, is there exactly one kept night per beat, and do both
   characters' accounts agree with it on every event (not on interpretation)?
2. TIME: does the party's beat address advance by play, and does one parking move up to
   the day fold correctly — the day's line containing its beats?
3. THE CHECK: does one contest resolve once, with dice both seats recompute identically
   from the beat address and their handle, and does its consequence bite in the next beat?
4. COST: calls and tokens per turn, each arm.
5. THE FELT NIGHT: the judge's blind reading of both transcripts — which reads as one
   night, and where does each lose the thread?

**Jettison.** S stores nothing the world did not already hold except the seats' mirrors and
the keeper's personal keeps: delete those and the world is byte-identical. P is untouched
throughout. A trial that cannot be jettisoned clean is not run.

**Stop-gates.** Question 1 failing ends B at readings only — the night organ already proven
— and the character's home stays at the table. Questions 1 to 3 passing opens rung 2: the
mirror at a world coordinate, which is nearly the stream room it renders today at
`spine:now` addresses. Rung 3 is promotion and decides the character's home (Plan B1).

**Cost.** Two seats by six beats by roughly fifteen thousand tokens, plus the keeper's
folds and the judge: on the order of three hundred thousand tokens per arm, a few pounds
on Sonnet, one afternoon.
