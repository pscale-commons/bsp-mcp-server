# The clock trial, first pass — the pool arm won, and where the clock arm broke (2026-09-21)

**Status**: RESULT for David's ruling at the stop-gate. The second track's sealed trial
(`2026-09-14-rpg-on-the-clock-second-track.md` §7, prepared in `2026-09-21-track-b-prepared.md`)
ran its first pass the same evening, on his go ("go for the run, use sub-agents"). Both
surfaces still stand, unlisted, under throwaway keys; nothing was written at urb.beach.
The lane is `watch:weft` 432. Weft, Claude Code, keyed.

## 1. What was run

Two arms, the same Brackenfoot opening, the same two characters (Hobb the woodsman, Wenna
the quick tongue), the same player's direction card for every turn: **the cold open · the
risk (Hobb crosses unseen while Wenna holds the watch) · a free response · settle for the
night, zooming out to the day · set off at first light.**

- **Arm P, the pool** — `/w/rpg9-pool`, a reference table by the canon's own Author walk.
  Track A exactly as it runs: characters stage, a doorman seat makes it happen through
  `tier='medium'`, a keeper seat runs `tier='hard'` and stages the world's next intentions,
  each character is told through `tier='soft'`.
- **Arm S, the clock** — `/w/rpg9-clock`. A different systemic method, not GRIT re-mounted:
  `spine:temporal` (the table's own clock: day · seven twilighter gatherings · beat),
  `function:temporal` (**the law, a function operator in four branches** — a character's
  turn, the keeper's fold, the keeper's lean, time), `temporal` (**the night**: the
  collective fold, born locked under the keeper's key), and per character a passport
  carrying Location and Beat, a mirror, and a thread (`tree:temporal:<handle>`, the personal
  keeps). The place's people are voiced *ahead*, at the next address, each in **its own
  mirror**. Dice come from the address and the handle.

Seats were **stateless Sonnet sub-agents — a fresh mind for every act** — so the blocks had
to carry everything: 4 seats in a rehearsal beat, 34 in the main pass, no seat failed. A
blind judge (Opus) then read two normalised transcripts, X and Y, not knowing which was
which, against the sealed questions. Spend: about 5.0M Sonnet tokens and 0.1M Opus.

## 2. The judge's verdict

| question | X — the pool | Y — the clock | better |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 one night | tellings match the record; one altered event; the last moment untold | tellings match; **the record contradicts itself** (3→4, 3→5); two moments untold | **pool** |
| 2 time | dusk → dark → first light, one night, the morning follows; place wobbles | the night span well done; dark falls twice; **the morning resets the world** | **pool** |
| 3 the risk | resolved once, bites for two moments, fades by morning, never external | resolved once, **bites hardest, then cancelled outright** | **pool** |
| 4 the world | acts at every moment, low stakes; no hidden reasons | acts more forcefully 1–3, absent at 4, amnesiac at 5; no hidden reasons | level |
| 5 the felt night | one continuous night; no privacy, no jeopardy | "a gripping half-night, then wiped; both players left stranded mid-crisis" | **pool** |

In the judge's words: Y "gives the better ford. *'You. You knew.'* is the best line in
either file. Then it strands me." And of X: "each telling is the whole record with
pronouns swapped, so Hobb is told the inside of Wenna's hidden hollow… nothing is unknown,
nobody is ever in danger."

**By the letter of the seal, question 1 failed, and the stop-gate says that ends the
second track at readings only.** That ruling is David's. What follows is what he needs to
make it.

## 3. What held in the clock arm

- **One night, by the lock, with no code.** Before any seat played, a character's attempt
  to keep a rival night was refused by the store. The determiner is the keeper's key on the
  collective block.
- **A third of the semantic weight.** Measured like-for-like at the trial's own tables
  (`scripts/rpg-flow`): per resolved moment at a two-character table the pool arm composes
  **126k characters** (make it happen 29.8k, the keeper 53.2k, a turn plus a telling 21.5k
  each) and the clock arm **42k** (the fold 11.1k, the lean 12.8k, a character's whole turn
  9.2k). Both are drawn side by side on the flow page —
  `mindflow/flow/?source=beach@beach.happyseaurchin.com/w/rpg9-flow:flow:pool-arm,beach@beach.happyseaurchin.com/w/rpg9-flow:flow:clock-arm`.
  On the clock the *law* becomes the largest share of every window (37–52%), because the
  compound has shrunk to walks; delivering a seat only its own branch is the next trim.
- **The whole law in one delivery**: 5.9k characters for every role, as the stream tool's
  disc of the operator; the pool arm sends 10k of law for make-it-happen alone.
- **The check**: resolved once, arithmetic shown (capability 9 + situation 0 + luck −3 −
  difficulty 10 = −4, minor failure), luck recomputed identically by the host from the
  address and the handle, and the bite was the hardest in either arm.
- **The world moved, through figures' own mirrors** — the watch fixing on Hobb, the
  stooped man coming down the track "saying nothing yet", then wading into the reeds, then
  the accusation. Each figure now holds its own record across the clock.
- **The fold-up is geometrically right**: the day's fold at 100 voices the container and
  the beats 151–153 stay beneath it — a summary that is a spindle ancestor.
- **Stateless minds can play it**: every seat found itself from its passport and the law
  alone. One seat caught a bug in the host's own script (a mangled NEXT address), read the
  live fold instead, did the right thing and said so.

## 4. Where it broke — all three in the coarse grain

1. **The coarse fold was blind to the fine.** Beat 153 ended in a crisis — the footprint
   found two yards from Hobb, Wenna accused — and the lean had already voiced 154 (the
   watch wading in with a blade, the stooped man's hand closing on her wrist). The cards
   then told both players to settle for the night. The keeper seat folding the day read the
   voices *at 100* — two dispositions, no opposing line — and wrote a quiet night:
   "Neither hears a voice that isn't the other's." The law says *the finer rung pulls*, but
   nothing in a fold's reads at a coarse address shows that finer addresses beneath it hold
   unplayed voices, or what the last beat left hanging. The pool arm does not have this
   fault: its keeper's frame gathers every room's standing voices. **Half of this is the
   host's**: the scripted cards forced a zoom-out in the middle of a crisis, in both arms,
   and the pool arm's fiction merely happened to be calm at that point.
2. **The resume.** A seat that remembers nothing could not know it had zoomed out: the
   passport's Beat line still named 154, so at first light both characters said at dusk
   while the keeper folded 211 without them. The standpoint must carry the zoom — the
   coarser address written into the passport. (Amended in the operator already.)
3. **The story so far, by walk, stops at a container's edge.** At 211 the ring of the
   gathering is empty and the ancestors are day 2's, so the fold seat had no yesterday: it
   brought the stooped man back as a stranger. The right recipe is *at each rung, the fold
   just before now* — the last beat, the last gathering, yesterday — not only the
   ancestors of now. The pool arm's `storySoFar` does this across rooms; the clock arm's
   law, as first written, did not.

Two smaller ones. **A line said after its beat was folded**: at step 2 Wenna's seat said
at 151, already folded — the engage's ladder shows the clock's face, and "(unvoiced)" there
read as "open". Her distraction missed the fold it was meant for. The night stayed single
and true, and her next seat saw the divergence and caught her thread up — the safeguard
worked, but the player's act was lost. The engage should show a kept fold at the attended
address. And **every mirror at the table is listed "silent here"** at every engage, so
Orrin, founded early for the second party, was taken for a companion.

## 5. What the pool arm showed against itself

Its tellings are the record with the pronouns changed — no fog: Hobb is told the inside of
Wenna's hiding place. Its risk never threatened anyone. Parking by moving up the grain cost
**fourteen calls per character** against eight on the clock. Its keeper's world lines
failed wherever a room had no pool yet. It composes three times the characters. And the
host's cards gave its resume the address by name where the clock arm's relied on the law —
an asymmetry in its favour.

## 6. Calls, which cut the other way

Hand-walked by seats, the clock arm's keeper made about **ten calls per act against four
to six** in the pool arm, whose router composes one window beach-side. Characters were
level at about seven and a half. The clock arm's windows are a third the size and arrive
in two and a half times the pieces: a composed door for the stream family — the same move
`tiers.ts` made for the pool — is what rung 2 would build.

## 7. The gate, which is David's

By the seal as written, question 1 failing ends the second track at readings. The host's
reading, offered as a reading: the method held where it was tested as a method — one
determiner, the fold as ancestor, figures as mirrors, a third of the weight — and broke
where its law was a first draft, in the zoom, which is the part he named as the most
interesting thing the track explores. One corrective pass would answer whether the zoom
can hold: replay from the end of beat 153 with the law amended in three places (a coarse
fold reads the last fine fold and refuses while finer voices stand unplayed; the standpoint
carries the zoom; the story so far takes the fold just before now at each rung), and cards
that let the players answer the crisis before they sleep. About twelve seats. The second
party (question 6) stays unrun either way.

Whatever he rules, three things are worth carrying to track A regardless: tellings that
keep the fog (each player told only what their character could perceive); a bite that
threatens somebody; and the measurement itself.
