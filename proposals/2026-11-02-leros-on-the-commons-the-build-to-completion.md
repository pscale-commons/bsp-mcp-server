## 1. The answer in short

A seal also breaks what the beach does with content (appends, folds, synthesis). So the members' circle is a **read gate on a ground of the LERO's own**, and the only thing that goes through sealing is what must be hidden even from that ground's operator: each member's days (and, as a small group seal, the leaders' full notes). This is the July finding (`2026-07-20-private-community-beach.md`) and the shell-indexes proposal's gated beach (its 1.111113), now with a concrete place to try it: David's own beach at bringingtheflavour.com, stood up for real as the first with the gate.

3. **The days' seal is the first piece of work, and it is small.** Matthew has already found both gaps himself (community-recovery-design:Phenomemental 8.4): the sealing key is the three words the beach checks, and every member shares one salt. The fix is a second word kept on the phone, a per-person salt, and a re-seal on next open; about a day of Matthew's time, with a shared set of test vectors from weft so the app, the site and the router never drift.

4. **The ground for real members is not any beach on Vercel and Upstash.** Both hosts' processing terms bar special category data (who-answers 1.5), and a LERO standing up the same package under its own account inherits the same terms. So "each LERO its own beach" means a beach on a host whose terms allow health data, or a design where the days never leave the phone (pocket-beach). David's beach at bringingtheflavour.com proves the gate on his own content; it is not the ground for a real member of a LERO.

5. **The sequence** (§5): settle and tie this week; the seal and the bringingtheflavour beach in the first fortnight; the gate and the members' circle on that beach by the Leadership Group's meeting of 17 November; the yes, Forget me and the tombstone after; then The Forward Trust's data protection lead reads the pack, the constitution is adopted so a controller can be named, the ground is chosen, and only then real days. ## 4. The design in detail

### 4.1 Ring one: the days' seal

1. **A sealing word of the person's own**, set once, kept on the device beside the three words (the same `ME_KEY` store, never in any request), memorable first as David ruled (pool:Phenomemental 159; ways:key 6), long later when a password manager syncs. Matthew's condition holds: a member never meets two words at the door; the sealing word is set once and the app uses it. On a phone that forgets its storage, the word is asked again; a lost sealing word loses the days sealed under it, as lost words already do (leros-on-the-commons 1.17218).
2. **A salt of the person's own**: gray.js seals with the handle, not the place's address; `open()` already tries the handle after the address, so every day already kept still opens, and the next open re-seals it under the new word and salt and writes it back (one latched write per day, by the owner's own hand, nothing for the operator to do).
3. **The connector says when it seals under the latch** (coloured-stigmergy 1.8, GO from David and weft): bsp-mcp's `enc_secret` already exists; the change is the ack's line when it falls back to `secret`, so no door seals under the latch silently.
4. **One set of test vectors** shared by gray.js, vault.js, stack.html and keys.ts: seal in one, open in the others, both ways; kept in bsp-mcp-server `scripts/` and run as `smoke:gray-vectors`. This is the guard against the drift coloured-stigmergy 5 names, and it is weft's.
5. **The dates stay open** on the shared beach (a day's address is its date; mark:<name> carries the count): accepted for trial names, moot on a gated ground where the index and the blocks are members-only.

Done when, on a trial name: a day kept with the new seal cannot be opened by the connector called with the three words, nor from the store with the three words and the place's address; a day kept before the change opens in the app and is re-sealed on its next open; the vectors pass in all four places.

### 4.2 Rings two and three: the gate on a ground of the LERO's own

**Why not a seal.** A members' circle sealed on the shared beach would still list every member's name in the place's index, in arrivals, in every `<x>:<name>` block, with born and touched; the ring at every group block's 9 names its members; and a sealed room cannot be appended to, so rooms, arrivals and the noticeboard would stop working. Matthew's "half-private is not private" (leros-phase-two:Phenomemental 1.118) is exactly right. The circle is a perimeter.

**The gate**, in the pscale-beach package, behind a switch so the open commons is byte-for-byte unchanged:

1. **On** by env `BEACH_GATE=members` (like `BEACH_CAPS`), honoured only when the deploy's own `settings` block stands behind a root latch, as the webhook line is today.
2. **The credential**: a member proves their passport's latch once (`POST` `{block: 'passport:<handle>', secret}` with no content already proves words and changes nothing); the handler answers with a short-lived token (HMAC over handle and expiry under a server secret, as `POOL_WEBHOOK_SECRET` is held), which the client sends on every GET as a bearer header. No session store, no cookie, no new block.
3. **What a non-member is served**: the index only as the subset marked beyond; a block or position only where its holder's index card says `beyond` at field 5 (shell-indexes 1.111113: `index:<holder>`, the entry whose line names the block, its 5); `self` and `beach` refused with one plain line. The holder of a bare block (lighthouse, spine:constitution) is the keepers' handle, whose index lists it. Cached a minute per holder, as settings are.
4. **Until shell indexes stand** on a beach, the keepers' own `index:<keepers>` is the one list the gate reads; founding it is one write (shell-indexes 1.112). So the gate waits on nothing but that proposal's names (its 1.1118).
5. **The clients**: the app sends the credential (it holds the words); bsp-mcp forwards `secret` on a read when a beach answers 401, so an LLM app reads a gated beach as its member; the button already holds the key in its frame.
6. **Ring three's full notes**: a group block sealed to the leaders' published halves (`members` in the router; the app opens it on the phone with the leader's sealing word, as stack.html does), rewritten whole, small by design; or, once sealed log appends land in the connector (coloured-stigmergy 1.11112), a log of its own. Each leader publishes their halves once (the app can do what `pscale_key_publish` does: Argon2id from the sealing word, salted with the handle, the halves at the passport's 9).

**Where it is tried**: beach.bringingtheflavour.com (§6), David's own beach, with Community Recovery's place copied there under trial names as a world of its own beside his blocks. Nothing of the live place moves.

### 4.4 At write: the yes, the guard and the notice

### 4.7 Block or reader: the scenario Matthew asks for

A member keeps Tuesday's day. The **block** is sealed: whoever gets hold of it, the beach's operator, a backup, a crawler, has ciphertext, because the key is on the member's phone. The **reader** is gated: a stranger who asks the LERO's beach for the member's card, room or the index gets nothing, because they hold no member's credential, while a member gets everything in the circle, in plain words, and the place's appends and folds work. "Reader" means whoever is at the door, a person in the app or an AI through the connector, and the gate checks the credential, never the words. Sealing protects a block against anyone; the gate protects a place against outsiders. A recovery place needs both, and needs each only once.

### 4.8 Resolution

David's "hold X, don't go into X minus one" stands in the design: the organisation reads the fold at the scale of the community and never a member's day; a leader reads the summary layer of a meeting and the full notes only as a leader; the national picture reads a LERO's fold and never a member. Each ring reads one scale coarser than the one beneath it, which is the geometry doing the work.

### 4.9 Cost

No LLM in any gate, seal or yes. Argon2id at 64 MB runs once per visit per key on the phone (under a second on a recent phone, a few seconds on an old one), cached for the visit; secretbox is milliseconds. The gate adds one cached block read per GET and one HMAC check. The group seal for the leaders' notes adds one unwrap per visit. The only paid minds in the design are the crossing agent and the meeting room's holder, on the commissioner's key, both outside privacy's path.
