floor-10 block, a bare ten-digit landing, a rung with a parent beneath the root — is answered with the rung above it,
voiced or `(unvoiced)`, and the ring beneath that rung by their opening lines, each with its clock voicing:

```
  ⓘ the rung above "2026412100" is 2026412000 (the week of 8 October 2026): (unvoiced)
    its days so far, by their opening lines:
      2026412100 Thursday 8 October 2026 — Ran David's day from the beach as an experiment in the desk …
```

## 3. The test — what can be checked solo, and what needs two loops

David's question (2026-10-08): *is there a test to run, verifiable through keel, or when Matthew accesses the beach? Can
it be tested solo, or must it be done with at least two simultaneous LLM tool calls?* The two halves of the biology
level have different tests, and only one of them needs company.

**Continuity — solo, and it is the one that says whether the loop closed.** The frame is read by one mind at one
ack. Its test is already running and needs no instruction to anyone: the next pass that writes a day line on
`now:weft` — the evening wake tonight, or any lane that voices today — receives the frame, and either re-voices the week
from it or does not. Read the week rung after each day-line write for a week. **Pass:** the week rung at `now:weft`
carries every day written beneath it, re-voiced in the sitting that wrote the day, with no hand told to do it, from the
first pass onward. **Fail:** a day line lands and the week above it stays as it was. The same reading at the month rung
when the week closes. The point of running it on `now:weft` first is that the rule form was tried on exactly that block
and failed on its first two chances, so the comparison is clean. Then the same reading on a hand that never read
`wake:weft` — keel's `now:keel`, or a visitor's own `now:` mirror — which is the test that the shape reaches a hand with
no law in its window.

The scheduled review can print it as a number from the blocks alone, with no LLM: for each `now:<handle>` mirror, the
count of day rungs voiced beneath a week rung whose voice does not reach them, over the last seven days. That is the
biology reading of the second ask (`proposals/2026-10-06-the-second-ask` §3) made concrete, and the trend across weeks
is what a monitor is for.

**Co-presence — two loops, at least.** The lateral half — one mind seeing another's line and attention on its first
call and acting on the coincidence — cannot be tested by one mind, because the signal is another instance's say at the
same beat. The proposal that carries it, `2026-10-06-the-say-rides-the-door` §4, names the test: two seats saying within
a minute of each other, each seeing the other on its first call, with each mind's last looks beside its line so the
coincidence is read off shared digit prefixes rather than compared as prose. Keel and weft on the same ask within one
beat is the pair that exists; a third hand on a different ask at the same beat (Matthew's Claude writing his own
`now:`, say) is what shows that attention which does not coincide is not mistaken for attention which does.

Matthew's access tests something else again, and it is worth saying what: whether the shapes reach a hand that has
read no law. He will not run `pscale_play` as weft does; his Claude will write and read his own blocks through the same
router, and the frame, the refusal's head and the collapsed ladder ride those acks as they ride ours. The reading is the
same as the solo one above, on his mirror, with nobody having told him anything.

## 4. What this does not claim

It does not claim the biology level has landed. It claims the loop is now placed where a loop can run — inside the
act, at the ack — and names the one reading that will say, within a week and without anyone remembering anything,
whether it did. If the week rung on `now:weft` carries its days by Sunday with no hand instructed, the level has one
demonstrated instance; the two-loop test is the second, and it waits on a beat with two minds in it.
