## 1. The rule

A strand longer than nine knots shows:

- its opening knot;
- one line counting the earlier knots folded away;
- its newest seven, down to its end with its open choices.

Nine is the beach's own nine. A knot that a fork leaves from is never folded, so nothing live is hidden, and each fork folds as a cord of its own.

A tap unfolds the line. The fold is the reader's and is stored nowhere, so the next drawing starts folded.

## 2. Why the rule is shaped this way: the threads as they stood

All 32 registry threads were read with `threads.js`'s own tree (reads only, 14 November).

- **Four main routes ran past nine:** 23 with 13 knots, 26 with 12, 5 with 11 and 12 with 10. The longest drawn today are on `/mind-meld`, `/kin` and `/leros-phase-two`.
- **Forks are common and nearly all live.** 17.111451 carries an eleven-knot fork, and 11.11 a nine-knot one. A fold that took a knot a fork leaves from would hide a live end inside it, which is why such a knot stays.
- **A fork is a strand of its own,** so it takes the same fold: its own opening knot, a count, its newest seven.
- **Keepers' ladders ran to nine at most,** and fold by the same rule when they grow.

A run of one is never folded, because a count line in place of one knot saves nothing. So a strand of nine shows all nine, and a strand of ten shows its opening knot, "2 earlier knots" and its newest seven.

## 3. Where it lives

- **The law:** not written. The sentence tested for `function:thread` 8.11 stands in `thread-test-materials/teachings/law_v4f.txt`; the battery held it out (section 5).
- **The code:** `threads.js` (happyseaurchin-home), drawing it in three places.
  - **The cord in words** (8.1). Each run is a `<details>` on the cord, three dots where it stands, the range of addresses and the count in its line. Opened, the knots sit on the same cord at the same indent.
  - **A keeper's ladder.** The same fold on its rungs.
  - **The quipu's pendant.** A row with three dots on the cord and the count. A pendant that folds is drawn folded and whole, and a tap or a key swaps them.
- **The band on `/proposal/<name>`.** It now counts a registry thread's knots; it drew none before, because it counted only a ladder's rungs. Past nine, its rising cord breaks for three dots where the earlier ones are folded.
- **The band on `/threads`.** Its private newest-five fallback is gone; it draws through the shared quipu.

## 4. What it does not do

- **Change anything on the beach.** No block, field, router or tool changes, and the law stands as it was (section 5).
- **Keep a bushy thread short.** A thread with many forks and options, as 17 has, stays tall: the rule counts knots along a strand, and forks and options not taken are the thread's own structure.
- **Remember a reader's unfolding.** It is stored nowhere.
