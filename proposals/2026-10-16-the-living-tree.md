## 1. What a person could not do

- **Add without replacing.** A hand's mirror at a part (`<V>-path:<handle>`) holds one line, revised by its
  holder. That suits where they stand and fails for ideas: a second suggestion writes over the first.
- **See the tree as it is now.** /beach-tree draws a snapshot of the spine taken on 14 October. Parts added
  since do not appear: the button at 4.6 and its four parts are missing.
- **Zoom to a part and read it**, in a pen of their choosing, or see across the whole path what waits on whom.

## 3. Three ways to add, each in the writer's own place

| What a hand adds | Where it lands | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Where a part stands from their side, or what they can bring to it | One line at the part in their own mirror `<V>-path:<handle>`, opening DONE, DOING, BLOCKED or OFFER. Saying it again replaces only their own line. | `function:path` 5 (standing) |
| A part, or several | The next free digit beneath the part in their own `spine:<V>-path:<handle>`. Each idea is a new address, so the second never covers the first. | `function:path` 1.3, `tree` 5.1 (standing) |
| Talk about a part over time: a need, a +1, a question, an idea not yet a part | A contribution to the project's own pool, located at the clock, naming the part as a card names it: `[<V>-path <address>]` | new: 5.2 below |

**3.1 Why talk is not located at the part's address.**
- `pool:beach-venture` sits on the venture's clock. Its voices are located at `spine:beach-venture` addresses
  such as 2026331700, because a pool is located against its own spine (tree 9).
- A located read compares bare digit runs (`digitsOfAddress` in `src/tools/pool.ts`; block-conventions 4.52).
  A path voice at `at='2'` would therefore be gathered with every voice on the 2026 clock, and a clock read
  at 2 would gather it.
- So the part rides as a pointer. It takes the form a card already carries at its 5 (`beach-venture-path 2.5`),
  which the fold and every view read today.
- The cost: a reader who wants one part's talk reads the pool and filters by pointer, where it could have asked
  the pool for a located slice. If that read grows heavy, the path takes a pool of its own (`pool:<V>-path`,
  located at its own addresses), and the project's pool keeps the clock.

**3.2 Named and passphrase only.**
- A mirror is born under its holder's key (`function:path` 8.4). A proposals spine is latched at its first
  write in the same way. No one else writes either.
- A pool contribution's slot answers to its contributor's secret (block-conventions 4.27). The name at a
  contribution's 1 is the contributor's own claim.
- A sed-membership pool also binds that name to a registration (4.27). Whether to make the pool one is the
  keeper's choice, for when it reaches people the venture does not already know.

**3.3 Popularity is read, never voted.**
- The count that matters at a part is how many distinct hands add there and beneath. Any reader's own LLM can
  weigh it from the three places above.
- The evening fold weighs what is said, not how often it is said.
- Nothing central counts, so a crowd costs the beach a few reads.

## 4. Growing and letting go

- **Growth follows the adds.** When the proposals, wordings and talk beneath a part outrun what its line can
  say, the folder proposes sub-parts in its own proposals spine. The keeper adopts them, appended and never
  renumbered (tree 5a).
- **Nine is the natural fold.** A part has nine branches, so its tenth part means a new level: the keeper groups
  the nine and opens a part above them, as an accumulator folds. Or the part graduates into a tree of its own,
  a path founded under `function:path` 8, with its old address pointing at it [spine:<project>] (1.4). A forest
  view would show it as a new tree.
- **Letting go keeps the address.** Addresses are permanent so that every mirror stays located (tree 5a). A part
  the keeper lets go keeps its address. Its line says it was let go and where its work went, the fold says so
  under its mark, and the views draw it bare.
