# Open days — a line may hold its holder open, and a standing word holds at every time

> **Status.** RULED AND BUILT 2026-10-29 (weft, through Claude Code, lane things.1), at David's word in weft's parlour, `pool:weft` 356 (28 October): *"WEDNESDAYS ARE MY OPEN DAY, and the availability law can't say it yet. Please make the changes it needs … How it's shaped is yours; what it has to hold is the words above."* Taken up the next afternoon at his *"Do them all, wednesdays first."* The law stands re-voiced on the beach; this is the router's half; the page is happyseaurchin-home `availability.html`, the invitation `invite.html`.

## 1. The ask

In his words: *"I'd like to make my availability on Wednesdays completely open, which means that I am free on Wednesday. Whether that happens to be for a group meeting or a solo individual one-to-one meeting, I am free for appointments on Wednesday. And obviously, only when I agree to a meeting will it get filled. But otherwise, I am free, which means that if I'm meeting somebody and somebody's meeting me, somebody else can pop in. That's fine too. So it's not a closed meeting."*

He had already put that word at the root voice of his own mirror, `availability:happyseaurchin`, and cleared a Wednesday `busy` a calendar sync had said. Five things the law could not carry:

1. A line only ever meant *out of reach*, so nobody could say **open** — and Matthew's *"Free 10:00–14:00 Europe/London only; working otherwise"* read as the opposite of what he meant.
2. A standing pattern, *every Wednesday*, has no cell, and a read at a span never delivered a holder's root.
3. The fill had to honour it: the recurring Sqale Open Meeting would be said `busy` again by the next sync.
4. A meeting on his open day stays open: it stands in his now with its link, and its own page should say it is open to whoever turns up.
5. The read and the fold should offer a time inside an open meeting as *join it*, not as a near miss.

## 2. The shape — words, and one rung that was always there

- **A line says what its holder's time is** (`function:availability` 1). Most take them out of reach; a line may as well hold them open — *"open"*, *"free 10:00–14:00, working otherwise"*, *"open meeting, anyone may join"* — and is read for what it says. Still read, never parsed.
- **The standing word is the mirror's root voice** (1.2). The root is the rung above every address of the clock, so what it says is true of each: *"Wednesdays are open"*, *"never before 10"*, *"away every August"*. The label a door wrote there at the mirror's birth is not the holder's word; what they write beside it or in its place is. A line at a cell still rules its own cell. David's word was already in the right place: nothing of his moves.
- **The fill honours it** (3.2). Before it says a commitment, the holder's AI reads the standing word, which every say now hands back, and says nothing out of reach on a time it holds open: a meeting there that the holder has made open is said as open, with the ring or link they made public; anything else is left unsaid.
- **A meeting on an open day stays open** (8.1). It stands in the holder's now with its link, anyone may join, and in availability it is said as open — so a read finds it, and the fold offers it as one to **join** (5), never a near miss.
- **An open meeting** (`function:meeting` 3). A Who that opens with the word *open* — `WHO: open — whoever turns up` — makes the meeting open: anyone may come, its invitation says so and asks nobody to be added, and booking it (7.1) takes nobody out of reach.

## 3. The rule the door learns

A family whose law voices a position, to its second rung, opening **THE STANDING WORD** keeps one (`standingLaw`). In such a family:

- **Read across a span**, each holder's standing word comes first, then their lines; a holder with a standing word and no line there is listed with it, not folded into *nothing said*.
- **A say** hands the holder their own standing word back, so a calendar synced by their AI meets it at every line it says.
- The standing word is the mirror's root voice with a birth label taken off: the door's own (`mirrorBorn`, the same text the page founds with) and the beach's `<name> at <host>.` (`standingWordOf`). Nothing reads the holder's words.

A family whose law says nothing of it reads exactly as before — `now`, whose roots hold life-scale lines, is untouched.
