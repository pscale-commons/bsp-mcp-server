## What stood

The look layer of 24 October (`pool:weft` 286, built in #535) made a look a block of named tokens and nothing else. It asked for `function:look` as the law; the law was never founded. Four faults in the page's resolver, found by the bee (`watch:weft` 771):

- it read only `key: #hex`, so `look:Phenomemental`, written `ground #eee7d5`, changed nothing;
- Cormorant Garamond and JetBrains Mono were on the allowlist but never loaded;
- the measure at 3 was never read;
- the law's `look: accent #hex` skipped the contrast check, so leros-phase-two's crimson stood at 3.1:1 on the dark ground.

Tokens alone cannot carry a design. Matthew's page draws its argument as framed ink figures whose marks mean one thing each. LERO.1 carried that into leros-phase-two by hand (happyseaurchin-home #580): its head comment names the hand it borrowed, and no block held it.

## What is written

**`function:look`**, the law, under the shared project lock:

- **1 the form.** `look:<handle>` keeps its tokens at 1 to 3, as before. It gains its grammar at 4 to 6: 4 the band and 5 the drawing (both already in 286's shape and in Matthew's block), and 6 the grammar, a new address. The underscore of 6 names the exemplar, the maker's own page that shows the look whole. Beneath it stand the rules a mind follows: the sheet, the frame, the marks with the one meaning each carries, the moves and the voice. 7 stays free, so a look is never a full fan.
- **2 worn by a page.** These are the rules `look.js` implements. Tokens go over `theme.css` and the grammar is never read. Ink and muted are checked at AA. An accent that fails is moved toward its ground's ink until it reads, keeping its hue, so a single `look: accent` stays the maker's colour in both themes rather than dropping to the standard teal.
- **3 the render**, the recipe, read down 3: read the family, read the look, draw this family in that hand, stamp it, keep it, tie it, hand it over. The page's craft stays the family's form's own (`ways:proposal` for a proposal), read by reference. The recipe says only what wearing a maker's look adds to that craft.
- **4 a render, read.** A render keeps its own copy of the spine's words, as the craft already asks. When it reads the spine live it marks each address whose words have moved since it was drawn, and says how many have. This is David's 1.11118, done by comparing the words the page was drawn with. No dates have to agree.
- **5 whose yes.** A look worn where its own 8 says needs no one's word. Worn anywhere else, it needs its maker's yes, asked by the family's keeper and recorded at the law's 6.

**The keeping.** The render a family wears is kept at `files:<name>`. Any other render of it, in another look or by another hand, is kept in that hand's own `files:<name>:<handle>`: a mirror at the render layer, so nobody writes over anybody's. Each render is tied as a rung on the family's page thread, `<name>-page`, under `function:thread` as it stands (threads.2 holds that form; nothing here changes it).

**`function:proposal` 4** gains one clause: where the law names a maker's look, the crafted page wears that maker's hand as `function:look` 3 says. Its drawings are still taken from what it proposes. "Never the maker's house look" now reads "never the maker's house look by habit". **`ways:proposal` 1** gains the same clause for the design bar. Its words before were:

> THE DESIGN BAR COMES FIRST — choose your own look, colours, type, graphics and animation; nobody needs to tell you this again. Before writing any code, name the page's world in one sentence, taken from what the proposal is about, not from who is making it: Time Nesting is rings of scale on a polar sundial; TRONIC Glass a Tron grid; Two Spines boro, patched indigo cloth joined by stitching, because it proposes joining two spines without merging them. Then decide everything at 2 to 7 from that world.

**`look:weft`** gains its 6: the standard page's own hand, with `/proposal/code-as-blocks` as its exemplar. Its weights move to the site's real 800 and 400, since reading the old "600 400" would have thinned code-as-blocks' headings. **`look:Phenomemental`** is Matthew's alone. His 6 is drafted from his own page, at `pool:Phenomemental`, for him to write, change or leave.

## The answer to David's question

Looks are the way, once a look carries its grammar. Code-as-blocks is how (`spine:code-as-blocks` 1.11): the renderer stays code, in `look.js` and the generic pages, and the authoring moves into blocks, in the look and the recipe. The LLM that draws a family is the compiler from block to page. The standard view of any family whose law says `look: <handle>` now wears that maker's tokens. A crafted render in that maker's hand can be drawn by any mind that reads `function:look` 3.

## The first test

The recipe was handed to a fresh mind (Claude, Opus 5.5, as a subagent of the lane). It got only the address `function:look` 3, the family `happening` (David's proposal, with no page and no look) and `look:Phenomemental` with his drafted grammar. It was barred from leros-phase-two and from every page in his look except the exemplar the draft names. It drew `happening.html`, 101,290 bytes. The world it named: the beach's days as knotted cords, each family born or room opened a knot tied at its stamp, and each day's headline the one knot never tied.

**Judged beside Matthew's page and leros-phase-two, it reads as his design.** It has:

- his paper and ink and Cormorant Garamond;
- the wordmark small and wide in capitals, and the headline's turn in crimson italic;
- every figure framed in a 1px line;
- counts drawn as rows of small ink squares;
- dashed boxes for what is not yet made: the day's headline and its best nine;
- hatching for what is sealed: three settings in David's own Vercel;
- crimson kept for the one cord that is today.

It was checked at 1280 light and 390 dark, with no sideways scroll and no script error, and its stamp sits in its head. A test copy with one drawn address altered said *"Drawn on 29 October 2026; the spine has been written since at one place"* and marked that section.

**Where it parted from the hand-made page, the recipe was silent.** It took Matthew's single column where LERO.1 had kept the craft's full width, because nothing said which of the two rules the layout. Its other findings:

- what a drawing does without the maker's yes;
- whether a crafted page loads `look.js` or carries its tokens;
- that his band was drawn for Sundial's own six places;
- that his mark at 5 signs his own pages;
- that the draft and his tokens gave two spacings for the wordmark;
- that the stamp had no room to say where the grammar came from.

Each is mended in `function:look` the same afternoon, at 3.1, 3.11, 3.111 and 3.1111. The founding text stands at `archive:function:look:2026-10-29`, and the draft at `stash:weft` 63 now defers to his tokens. The test also hit the trap keel has met three times, `theme.js` taking a page's own `.bar` for the site's bar, and `ways:proposal` 1.7 now names it.

The render is kept off the beach, so nothing stands at `files:happening` and no rung is tied, until Matthew says yes to his design on David's proposals; that yes is David's to ask. The page and the comparison shots stand in the lane group's notes folder, `look-1/`. The account is at `watch:weft`, under the lane look.1.
