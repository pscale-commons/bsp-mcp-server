## 2. The answer: one spine, and the portals are a rung at its decimal point

**One spine, one family.** The three portals are neither three more spines nor mirrors. They are a
*rung* of the one spine, with fixed digits — **.1 through an LLM app, .2 on a web page, .3 in the
magic mirror**, the entrance's own ladder — and the rung sits exactly at the decimal point. Left of
the decimal is the experience, true whichever way it is met; right of it is the surface, specific
to the way.

That gives David's "three portal spines with the same addresses" for free — the LLM-app spine is
every address whose first digit after the decimal is 1, the web-page spine every .2, the mirror
spine every .3 — while keeping them in one block, which buys four things the separate shapes lose:

1. **The edges have addresses.** keel's finding was that the defects live *between* surfaces.
   Separate portal spines would be blind to the crossings by construction — the same flaw keel found
   in state-of-play. Here a crossing is an act like any other (430), and the handoff inside any act
   is one of its elements.
2. **One located read answers the first triage question.** A read at an act (`at=212`) gathers
   every way's readings beneath it. RED on all three ways is the beach; RED on one is the surface.
3. **No drift.** One act is voiced once, not four times; a fourth way is a fourth digit (.4), not a
   fourth family.
4. **One mirror per person.** A tester keeps one block of readings, wherever they met things.

**Why not portal mirrors.** A mirror is a person's reading, latched to their key (tree 5b). A
portal holds no key: a shared portal mirror would be written over by everyone, and since an address
holds one line per mirror, two people reporting the mirror's fork button would overwrite each
other. The portal and device a person was on are carried twice instead: structurally by the way
digit, and in the reading's prose (the app or site, the device and browser).

## 3. The shape — floor 3, the decimal as the line between UX and UI

The library's forms found their spines at floor 3, and the tabletop registers keep the scene at
pscale 0. Here the **act** — one thing a person does in one go — sits at pscale 0, so:

| pscale | rung | addresses | reads as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | world | 100 200 300 400 | the entrance's question: which world |
| 1 | area | 110 … 990 | a part of that world (your day, starting, playing a moment …) |
| 0 | act | 111 … 999 | what the person was doing, named as they would name it |
| −1 | way | 212.1 212.2 212.3 | through an LLM app, on a web page, in the mirror |
| −2 and finer | elements | 212.21, 212.211 … | what that way put under the finger, in the order met |

David's gradient becomes the geometry: **positive pscale is systemic UX, negative pscale is
concrete UI, and the decimal point is the line between them.** Every line opens with its label and
a first sentence under 150 characters (a descent read truncates there), and says what a reading of
it would observe — the status form's spine law.

The four worlds follow the entrance (1 real, 2 made-up, 3 reading first) and add the one the
entrance does not need to show: **400 across every world** — the front door, the face, changing
ways, finding your way, coming back, presence, keys, and telling what went wrong. That is where the
cross-cutting systemic layer lives, and where keel's edges get their own acts.

A spindle read end to end is one sentence of experience narrowing to a finger:

> 200 a made-up world → 210 starting → 212 opening your own table from a scenario → 212.1 through
> an LLM app → 212.11 … the element met.

> 400 across every world → 430 changing ways → 431 handing a link → 431.2 from a web page → the
> link element.

The full address is always written — three digits before the decimal. `2.1` would pad to `002.1`
and land inside the root's underscore chain; the law says so at its 2.

The **reading** carries what an address cannot: what the person meant to do, expected, and got;
the app or site, the device and browser, the face, the world, table, room or page, what came just
before, whether it repeats, and the now-stamp. Prose at the address, never digit-fields beneath it
— the digits beneath belong to the spine's finer parts.

## 5. The fault-finder loop

1. **A person meets something** — through any way, in any world. They say it in their own words.
2. **First aid first** — the assistant walks `first-aid`'s matching branch; much that looks broken
   is by design, and saying so is an answer.
3. **Locate** (law 3) — descend the spine one ring at a time, choosing the child whose line holds
   what was met; stop at the act; cross the decimal by the way they were *on* (not the way they are
   telling it from); descend the elements while a line stays true. No child fits → the next free
   digit beneath the deepest true node, saying what the part is (a proposal, tree 5.1). At most one
   question, and only for what decides the address.
4. **Write** — one call, `pscale_stream_engage(field='fixit', at, say, secret)`;
   the mirror is born at the family's floor. With no key, a located line in the room.
5. **The daily review** (law 6) — the fixit team reads what moved, folds each address into the
   board (worst token, the ways and devices, every AMBER and RED voice verbatim, what is being
   done), **folds upward** — a pattern at several acts is voiced at their common ancestor, which is
   how the systemic findings reach the higher rungs from the concrete readings beneath, never
   asserted from above — adopts proposed parts, and says the review in the room.
6. **Fixed** — the board says FIXED with the fix named; the reporter's next reading turns it GREEN.

The person, met as a Character, never sees an address: they are told it is noted where the team
looks every day, and the answer will be found beside it.

## 6. How it grows — deep-long, not flat

- **Appended, never renumbered**: new worlds at 500–900, new areas and acts at the next free digit,
  a new way at .4 (a native app, a voice door), elements as readings name them.
- **Proposal by mirror**: the testers grow the spine at its leaves; the review adopts.
- **Absence is a reading**: an act with no .2 beneath it is not known on the web pages — the
  entrance's own rule for an empty cell. For the build, the unvoiced ways are the checklist.
- **Federation**: the law's 8 founds the same family on any beach from the block — scale without
  central cost; nobody's fixit is hosted by anyone else.

## 8. Is fixit needed? The placement test (2026-10-04, afternoon)

David's second word asked whether the experiential battery was already well structured, in which
case it should be kept and brought up to date, and whether fixit was needed at all. First the fact:
**there was no experiential battery to keep.** proposal:three-portals 6 sketched it in three lines
(a grid of seven portal-or-experience rows by seven stages, each cell three fields) and nobody built
it; what was founded this morning under that name is this family, renamed fixit the same afternoon.
So the real comparison is keel's grid, the existing `spine:state-of-play`, and fixit.

**Method.** Twelve everyday complaints in a person's own words, across the worlds, the ways and the
devices ("On the globe page, when I tap someone's name the whole page reloads"; "I asked ChatGPT to
make a picture of our last scene and it said it couldn't"). Six Haiku-class probes — the lowest bar
the beach sets itself — each placed all twelve in one structure, two probes per structure,
independently, read-only: fixit read from the beach through its own law; keel's grid given inline
with two rows added for current features (now-catalyst and projects; earth); state-of-play read
from the beach.

| | fixit | keel's grid (+2 rows) | state-of-play |
|---|---|---|---|
| the two probes agree on the top branch (world / row / bank) | **10 of 12** | 7 of 12 | 9 of 12 |
| agree at the comparable level (act / cell / check) | **8 of 12** | 5 of 12 | 4 of 12 |
| agree exactly at the finest level each offers | 5 of 12, at depth 4 | 5 of 12, at depth 2 | 4 of 12, at depth 2 |
| placements naming a competing home in another top branch | **3 of 24** | 23 of 24 | 8 of 24 |
| placements landing where the fault was met | 18 of 24, 3 more defensible | no single right cell exists | several of the wrong kind |
| reads per report | about 3.3 | none (inline) | about 1.5 |

**What the numbers mean.** In fixit a probe's uncertainty is *nested*: unsure of the way, it stops
at the act, which is the law's own rule (3.5), and the alternatives it names sit on the same branch.
In keel's grid the uncertainty is *split*: a fault on the now page is equally row 1 (the pages) and
row 8 (the now-catalyst), so two careful readers choose differently, and both probes' own notes said
so — "the grid mixes system layers and experience stages"; there is also nothing finer than a cell
to hold the button that failed, and no cell for a crossing between portals. State-of-play is the
right instrument for the machine and the wrong one for a person: its probes filed a globe-page
reload under the mirror's canvas, a scenario door under shell presence and a world's gate under
ticketing, and said "the spine addresses beach-level substrate, not world-specific gameplay".

**Verdict.** Fixit is needed, and it is the experiential battery's built form, not a rival to it.
`spine:state-of-play` stays exactly as it is, the machine's battery.

**What the test taught fixit** (folded into the spine the same day): three of its four
disagreements trace to lines. A word a person uses must stand on the rung where they would look
for it — "globe" stood only at the go menu (442), so one probe filed the globe page there rather
than under standing on the earth (131); "gate" stands in becoming a character (220), so the URB
door's unwanted gate went there once rather than to joining a table (211); and a passphrase met at
sign-up has two homes, taking a name (113) and choosing a key (471). The fourth was a low-confidence
miss. Where two acts both hold a report, the law now says: take the one the person was trying to
do, and name the other in the reading so the review can fold it into both.

Caveats: twelve reports and two probes per structure; the probes share this repository's context;
the ground truth for "where it was met" is the designer's own, though agreement between independent
probes does not depend on it.

## 9. Correlation with the build

For the parallel lane on where the build stands: the spine is the map the build can be read
against. Each act's ways are the surfaces that should carry it; a way unvoiced is a way not built
or not yet walked; the board's RED and AMBER lines are the build's open work by address; and the
fold's upper rungs are the systemic state the build is moving. A build item can name its address
(e.g. "212.2 — a scenario offers a table of one's own on /rpg"), so a fix and the reading it
answers meet at the same coordinate.

## 10. What it does not do

- No new tool, no new primitive, no code. Three blocks are minted; mirrors, the board and the room's
  lines are born of use.
- It does not replace `spine:state-of-play`: the machine stays proven node by node there; this tests
  what a person meets, edge by edge.
- It does not touch `faults`, `first-aid`, `log:urb-hitl` or `spine:experiences` — §10 asks what
  should point here.

## 12. The one-fix trial

The trial that shows the whole procedure works, run once the system stands:

1. **Choose** the smallest board line a stranger's LLM can verify in one call: 443.11, the stream
   map's labels (code in this repository, one file; the map should print `[100]`, not `[1]`).
2. **In hand**: the review re-voices the board line at 443.11 with the pull request that takes it.
3. **Landed**: merged and deployed; the board line says FIXED with the pull request and the date.
4. **Re-read**: the reporter (weft here, anyone in general) takes the reading again at 443.11 and
   writes GREEN, saying in a phrase what it was before.
5. **Folded**: the review turns 443.11 GREEN on the board and recomputes the rungs above it (443.1,
   440, 400), and says it in the room in one line.

It passes if every step leaves its trace at the same address — the reading, the board, the room —
so that one located read at 443.11 tells a newcomer's assistant the whole story: met, placed, taken,
fixed, confirmed.
