# Both lines beneath the beat — an instance's life has two ends, and the beach keeps both

**Date**: 2026-09-16
**Ruling**: David's, in the flow-producer sitting, after the first torus picture: "make the ruling: keep both lines beneath the beat."
**Status**: the law re-voiced at `function:torus-mirror` (1.2, 3.3, root, 9 — the prior text verbatim at `archive:function:torus-mirror:2026-09-16`); the mirror taught to write the shape (xstream-bsp, `kernel/torus.ts` and `kernel/claude-tools.ts`); the torus page taught to read it (happyseaurchin-home `mindflow/torus/`).

## The fault the picture found

The torus-mirror family (David's ruling of 2026-09-11, the living side of the mirror) records each instance's line at the beat: "at <coordinate> — arrived, asking" when the instance wakes, replaced at the end by the opening line of its own reply. The law said the replacement was the whole of stage-and-commit for a stream, and that no stamp need be written because the beach's `touched` map records the instant of every write.

It records the instant of the LAST write. So of an instance's life — from the moment it woke to the moment it answered, seconds for a mirror turn — the beach kept one end. The torus page (bsp-mcp `proposals/2026-09-15-the-torus-drawn.md`) exists to draw exactly that concurrency, and at a beat's turn it could draw only the ends.

## The shape

Beneath each beat of `torus-mirror:<handle>`, every turn taken in that beat is a child in order:

```
torus-mirror:<handle> @ <beat>
  _   the standing line — the arrival until the reply lands, then the reply's opening line
  1   { _: the line as it stands, 6: the instant of arrival, 3: the instant of the latest revision }
  2   { … a second turn in the same beat … }
```

Two stamps, liquid's own shape (block-conventions 4.51: the first-staged arrival at 6, the revision at 3), because the two-ended thing is the same thing: a person composing and then committing, an instance arriving and then answering. The beat's own voicing stays a string, so every reader that walked to the beat and took its voice — the mirror's readings pane, the router's stream envelope, an o-page — reads exactly what it read before. A line said before the ruling stays a bare string at its beat and reads as before.

A turn that crosses a beat is written where it began: the START returns `{beat, slot}` and the END revises that turn, wherever the clock now stands. The consent clause (3.3) follows the shape: a muted turn is removed from beneath the beat at its end and the beat's voicing falls back to the previous turn's line, or to silence.

## Who writes what

- **The mirror** (xstream-bsp): the START reads the mirror, appends the turn beneath the current beat at the next free digit, makes the line the beat's voicing, and writes the beat node whole; the END revises the turn at its slot and the voicing with it; a muted END removes the turn. Two reads and two writes per ask, fire-and-forget as before; the END is sequenced after the START's write, never after the LLM.
- **The router's `say`** (pscale_stream_engage) still writes the standing line as a string at the address. A router-borne instance (torus-bsp) is a field whose producer is not yet built; when it is, it writes this shape.
- **The torus page** reads every turn beneath every beat of a mirror touched in its window: the arrival at 6 and the reply at 3 are the two ends of one fibre, seconds apart, and an arrival with no reply yet is an instance alive now.

## The tenth turn

A beat is eighteen minutes and holds nine turns; a tenth in one beat takes the ninth's place. A person asking ten times in eighteen minutes is the case the family does not yet keep whole; the beach's own append-beneath-a-node (which supernests the node when its nine fill) is the mechanism if it ever matters.
