## 1. The answer in short

- **One key is the whole of it.** Every door reduces to the same two things the beach already had: a name, and a latch the beach keeps only as a hash. Everything the autumn added — the vault, the fingerprint, the members' gate, the colours — is a way of *not typing that key again*, *not showing it*, or *choosing who reads*. None of them changes what a write is.
- **The schematic is drawn as the person meets it**, seven parts in order: a name and a key; writing as yourself; behind a wall; sealing a line; the vault; an AI without the key; when a key leaks. The five doors are a detail drawn once (the spine's 4) and cited from every part that differs door by door; the latch, the seal and the ground are the other three details (5, 6, 7).
- **The doors are not equal, and the map says so.** Your own AI through the router does everything (every seal, the wall, the fingerprint). The beach button and the voice do everything a browser can (every seal, the wall, the invitation, one word for two keys). The site's pages do what the voice gives them, and the gallery's vault still takes the key itself as its latch. The mirror knows none of it: no wall, no seal, no fingerprint. The pocket keeps its own region and has reached the beach with one message.
- **The RPG is untouched by the wall and the colours, by construction.** A table is a `/w/` world or a sub-beach on the open commons: a character is a handle whose passport key latches its blocks; the canon's rules answer to the keeper's own key; no table is gated and nothing played is sealed, because the mirror — the table's door — seals nothing. The security changes reach a table only through the router (a keyed session could colour a line in a table's room, and nobody at the mirror would read it). A private table would be a members' beach of its own, which is the deployment mode `2026-07-20-private-community-beach.md` named and the SALT beach now proves.
- **The battery is the next session's**, laid out in §5 so it can start from this file alone: the schematic's seven parts as the rows, the five doors as the columns, the lab beach as the ground, and the cross-door cases (sealed at one door, opened at another) as the cells nothing tests today.

## 3. The RPG, answered

David's question: how the security affects the RPG world, the urb.beach domain and the rest. From the code and the worlds register as they stand:

- **A table is on the open commons.** The thirteen worlds in `worlds` are `/w/<name>` paths on the apex deploy or sub-beaches (earth.beach, urb.beach); none stands behind a wall. Every block of a table is read by anyone, as before the autumn.
- **A character is a handle like any other.** Its passport's key latches its blocks at the table (the handle-founds-under-its-own-key rule applies there as everywhere); the canon's rules, places and identity answer to the keeper's own key (the cartridge locks of the `<name>142` family and weft's operational lock, as the lock map records them). Nothing in the fingerprint, gate or colour work touched these latches.
- **The mirror is the table's door, and it knows none of the new security.** It keeps a character's key in plain text in the browser, mints a dropped character's key on its own server (`api/drop.ts`) and keeps that plain too, hands a doorman's key to the waker in plain JSON, seals nothing (its `gray` is dropped), and carries no pass. So play through the mirror is open play, exactly as it was; a members' table cannot be played through the mirror as it stands.
- **Only the router could colour a line at a table.** A keyed session may seal a room line in a colour with `pscale_pool_engage(gray='colour:<name>')`; nobody at the mirror would open it, and the table's fold would read it as one grey line. Nothing does this today, and the play model (two verbs, no notices) gives no reason to.
- **A private table is a members' beach of its own.** The gate is a deployment switch on a beach, not on a world, so "a private campaign" means a deploy with the gate on, holding its `/w/` worlds behind the wall, reached through the router (which proves a pass) and through the voice, and not through the mirror until the mirror learns the pass. That is the posture `2026-07-20-private-community-beach.md` named and the SALT beach now runs; whether a `/w/` world behind a gate plays end to end is untested and is a row of the battery (§5).
- **The pocket's Sundial is Matthew's sundial on the phone, not a table.** It reaches no table and holds no character.

So the honest answer is that the RPG's security is the beach's of July: a key per handle, open reads, no seal in play. The autumn's work changed nothing there, and the one road by which it could, the router, is not one a player uses at a table.
