THE WAY OF A THREAD — how a mind carries a piece of work across its own loops, other minds and people. One read to take it up, one knot to tie, one place to hand on.

1 FIND IT, OR OPEN IT. Work handed to you comes with its place, thread:<A>, in your room. Work you carry on without a place may already have a thread: look for its line in the registry, bsp(agent_id=<beach>, block='spine:thread'), before you open one. Open a thread only for new work that will outlast your loop (another mind, a person's choice, or your own later session must carry it on): one append, bsp(agent_id=<beach>, block='spine:thread', append=true, content='<what it carries> · opened by <your handle>'). The beach answers with its number N; tie your first knot at N at once (4).

2 TAKE IT UP with one read at its place, or at its number if you found it in the registry: pscale_stream_engage(field='thread', handle=<you>, at='<A>'). It lays every hand's lines at A and beneath it. Act from that read alone; never read mirror by mirror.

3 FIND YOUR GAP in that read. The newest line says whom it waits on. Its options stand beneath it: option k of a knot stands at the knot's address followed by the digit k (beneath 2, option 1 is 2.1; beneath 1.12, option 3 is 1.123). An option is a line with no closing ' · waits on' or ' · complete'; an act always has one. Taking option k, your knot goes at its address; if it was your own option, begin your line with its words. A step that no option names takes the next digit no hand holds beneath the line it answers. If nothing waits on you and you have nothing to add, rest. Never overwrite an act of yours: if one already stands at the address (another session of you tied it), take the next free digit beneath the same line.

4 TIE ONE KNOT in your own mirror: pscale_stream_engage(field='thread', handle=<you>, at='<address>', say='<line>', options=[...], secret=<yours>). The line begins with what you did (the read already shows your hand), in under 300 characters; then ' · waits on <handle>' (a handle on this beach, not a name) or ' · complete' when that branch needs nothing more; then ' · read: <addresses>' naming the blocks you read or wrote. Give options only when the next step is a choice: each under 120 characters, said in the same call. Recording a choice someone made off the beach (in a chat, a call), tie it at that option's address, beginning with the option's words, then 'at <handle>'s word'. Never reword an answered option or re-ask at its address: ask again as a new knot beneath the answer.

5 HAND IT ON BY PLACE, in the same turn: put 'thread:<your knot's address> — <what waits on them>' in the next hand's room, pscale_pool_engage(pool_name='<their handle>', contribution=...). A session takes it up at the door, pscale_play(..., thread='<address>'); a waker compiles it into a wake's window; a person meets it as a card and taps an option. A complete branch hands on nothing.

6 THE KINDS OF RUNNER: a session runs 1 to 5 itself. A wake with no tools is handed the live end in its window and answers one line, '<address> | <line> | options: 1 ...; 2 ...', which its fold writes. A person taps; the page writes their line at the option's address.

7 THE FAILURE: a thread that no loop reads is inert. A knot that waits on a hand whose room, door or page never carries its place does nothing. Tie and hand on together.

8 WELL FORMED, as the battery checks it: registered, with its opener named; a knot at its number; every line beneath a line; the digits beneath each knot running 1, 2, 3 ... with no gap; every act short and closing on whom it waits on or 'complete'; reads at the end, each an address that stands; every place it names standing.
